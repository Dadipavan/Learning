12.6 Multiple Imputation (MICE) and Oversampling (SMOTE), Tested Honestly

Two popular fixes appear in every tutorial: fill the missing values with a model (MICE), and fabricate extra rare-class rows (SMOTE). Both can help and both are often used wrongly. This section runs each against a clear baseline.

MICE: impute using the other columns, several times

Mean imputation pastes the same number into every hole, which flattens the column (smaller variance) and weakens its correlation with everything. MICE (multiple imputation by chained equations) instead regresses each incomplete column on the others, fills the holes with the predictions, and cycles through the columns until the fills stop changing. In scikit-learn it is IterativeImputer. The "multiple" part: do it m times with random draws added (sample_posterior=True), analyse each completed data set, and combine with Rubin's rules: the total variance is W + (1 + 1/m) B, where W is the average within-imputation variance and B the variance of the estimates between imputations.

Test: 1,000 people with age and income (income = 2 × age plus noise), 30% of incomes deleted at random.

Problem 12.6.1 Read what the output says about mean versus iterative imputation, and compute the Rubin total standard error.

Step 1RMSE on the hidden incomes: mean imputation 19.36 (that is about the spread of income itself, since it predicts the average for everyone), iterative 5.20 (close to the noise level of 5).
Step 2The imputed column's standard deviation: truth 20.48, mean-filled 17.64 (too small), iterative 20.37 (right). Correlation of age and income: truth 0.968, mean-filled 0.834 (attenuated), iterative 0.978.
Step 3Rubin with m = 5: W = 0.4238, B = 0.0035; T = 0.4238 + 1.2 × 0.0035 = 0.4280; sqrt(T) = 0.654.
AnswerThe standard error of the mean income is 0.654. Single imputation would have reported only sqrt(W) = 0.651 here, almost the same because the missingness is mild and predictable; with more missing data and weaker predictors, B grows and the gap matters.
Why it mattersFor prediction, a single IterativeImputer inside the pipeline is usually enough. For inference (confidence intervals, p-values) you need the multiple version, or you under-state uncertainty.

Note that the corr(age, income) of 0.978 in the iterative case is slightly above the truth: deterministic regression fills lie exactly on the line and have no noise. That is why sample_posterior=True is used for inference.

COMMON MISTAKE
Fitting the imputer on the full data before splitting. It learns from test rows, a leak (Chapter 8). Put IterativeImputer inside the Pipeline so it is fitted on training folds only.
COMMON MISTAKE
Imputing when the data is missing not at random (MNAR), such as high earners hiding income. No imputer can see what the other columns do not reveal. Add a missing-indicator column and discuss the risk with the data owner.

SMOTE: synthetic minority rows, and the protocol that makes it honest

SMOTE makes a new minority row by picking a minority point, picking one of its k nearest minority neighbours, and taking a random point on the line segment between them. It needs no library; the 8-line function in the code below is the whole algorithm (the imbalanced-learn package, not installed here, wraps the same idea). The experiment: 4,000 rows, 3% positive, a random forest, three ways to deal with imbalance. All numbers are measured on an untouched test set.

Problem 12.6.2 Which approach should you ship, and what does the last line teach?

Step 1Compare ranking quality (PR-AUC, threshold free): baseline 0.846, SMOTE 0.783, class weights 0.811. Nothing beat the plain model.
Step 2Compare the 0.5-threshold behaviour: SMOTE raised recall from 0.68 to 0.82 but dropped precision from 0.92 to 0.67. That is just the same ranking cut at a lower effective threshold.
Step 3So the baseline with a tuned threshold (Chapter 9) would reproduce SMOTE's recall-precision point without distorting the probabilities.
Step 4The last line: doing SMOTE before cross-validation gives PR-AUC 0.998. The synthetic copies of a test fold's positives sit in the training folds, so the model "recognises" them. It is a leak, and it turns a 0.85 model into an apparent 1.0.
AnswerShip the baseline plus a cost-based threshold. Use SMOTE only after comparing on a held-out set, and only inside the training fold.
Why it mattersOversampling changes the class prior, so predicted probabilities no longer mean what they say. If you need probabilities (pricing, risk), recalibrate on untouched data.
COMMON MISTAKE
Resampling the whole data set, then splitting or cross-validating. Always resample inside each training fold (in imblearn.pipeline.Pipeline, not run here, or by hand as above), and evaluate on the original distribution.

Check yourself

  1. Why does mean imputation weaken correlations? Answer: it replaces values by a constant, which has zero covariance with everything.
  2. When is SMOTE most likely to help? Answer: weak learners without class weights, very few positives, and continuous features where interpolation is meaningful; it is meaningless for categorical codes.
  3. What is B in Rubin's rules? Answer: the spread of the estimate across the m completed data sets, which captures the uncertainty due to missingness.
