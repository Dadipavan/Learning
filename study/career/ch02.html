CHAPTER 2

Learning How to Learn

You do not have an information problem. You have an energy and method problem. Fix the method and one hour a day goes much further.

You will study for years while you work full time, so the way you study matters more than the number of courses you buy. Many learners watch ten hours of video and remember almost nothing a week later. That is not a lack of talent. It is a method problem, and methods can be changed. In this chapter you will learn the study techniques that research supports, how to set up a one-hour session, how to stop jumping between courses, and how to protect your energy. By the end you will have a weekly routine you can actually keep.

2.1 Active Recall and Spaced Repetition

INTUITION
Imagine a school that gives its pupils one exam in September and never checks again. You would not trust that the pupils still know the material in June. Memory is the same. A fact you met once was "checked once". Each time you pull it out of your head on purpose, the memory gets stronger. Watching a video again is like re-reading the old exam paper. Recalling is like sitting a fresh quiz.

How it works

  • Active recall means you try to produce the answer from memory before you look. Example: close the notes and write what a LEFT JOIN does.
  • Spaced repetition means you repeat the recall with growing gaps: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 14 days, 30 days. You review just when you are about to forget.
  • Desirable difficulty means a little struggle helps. If recall feels easy, you may be repeating too soon. If you cannot recall at all, the gap was too long or the first learning was thin.
  • Anki is a free flashcard app that schedules the gaps for you. Use your own words on the card, one idea per card.

Good cards and bad cards

Problem 2.1.1 You learn "what is an index in SQL" on Monday 9 November 2026. Use gaps of 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days. When are the reviews?

Step 1Day 0 is Monday 9 Nov. Add 1 day: Tue 10 Nov.
Step 2Add 3 days: Fri 13 Nov. Add 7: Fri 20 Nov. Add 14: Fri 4 Dec. Add 30: Sun 3 Jan 2027. (Computed with Python.)
AnswerReviews on 10 Nov, 13 Nov, 20 Nov, 4 Dec and 3 Jan. That is 5 reviews, about 30 seconds each, so 2.5 minutes of work per card for a lasting memory.
CheckTotal days from learning to the last review: 1 + 3 + 7 + 14 + 30 = 55. From 9 Nov plus 55 days is 3 Jan. Correct. In plain words: 2.5 minutes spread over 55 days buys months of memory.

Problem 2.1.2 You add 5 new cards a day. How many cards after 60 days, and is it affordable at 10 minutes of Anki per day?

Step 15 x 60 = 300 cards.
Step 2Each card needs 5 reviews x 0.5 min = 2.5 min over its life. 300 x 2.5 = 750 minutes in total across 60+ days, about 12.5 minutes per day averaged over 60 days.
AnswerIt is slightly above 10 minutes. Reduce to 3 or 4 new cards a day, and write only cards for things you really need. Quality over quantity.
Check750 / 60 days = 12.5 min per day. At 4 new cards a day: 240 cards x 2.5 = 600 min; 600 / 60 = 10 min per day. Matches the budget.

Connection to the real job

SQL syntax, window functions, Python idioms, metrics definitions and system design numbers are all recall skills in an interview. Put the "numbers to remember" (e.g. 1 day = 86,400 seconds) on cards.

Applications

  • Interview prep (SQL patterns, statistics definitions).
  • Cloud exam certifications.
  • Learning a domain's vocabulary (Chapter 13).
COMMON MISTAKE
Making 50 cards from one video. Cards pile up, reviews become a burden and you quit. Cap new cards at 3 to 5 a day and delete cards you do not need.

Check yourself

  1. What is the difference between re-reading and recall? Answer: Recall produces the answer from memory; re-reading only recognises it.
  2. Name the review gaps. Answer: about 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days.
  3. Why one idea per card? Answer: You can tell exactly what you forgot.

2.2 Interleaving, Teaching Back and Deliberate Practice

INTUITION
A cricket batter who only practises one shot against one bowler is surprised in the match. Practising mixed shots is harder in the nets and better in the game. This is interleaving.

Three methods

  • Interleaving: mix problem types in one session instead of 10 of the same kind. Example: one SQL join, one window function, one Python dictionary problem, one probability question.
  • Teaching back: explain the idea aloud to an imaginary beginner (or write a short post). Gaps in your explanation show gaps in your understanding. This is also the Feynman technique.
  • Deliberate practice: work at the edge of your ability on one specific weakness, with fast feedback. Example: "I fail window-function questions" leads to 10 window problems, check each answer, write why you missed.

A one-hour session shape

MinutesActivity
0-10Recall: Anki reviews and "what did I learn yesterday" from memory
10-40Build or solve problems on today's topic (no copy-paste)
40-50Interleave: 2 old-topic problems
50-60Teach back aloud, then write 2 cards and 1 log line

Problem 2.2.1 You have 8 SQL practice problems: 4 joins, 4 window functions. Show an interleaved order versus a blocked order, and say which gives better learning.

Step 1Blocked: J J J J W W W W. You know the next problem type, so you skip the decision "which tool?".
Step 2Interleaved: J W J W W J W J. You must first recognise the pattern, as in a real interview.
AnswerInterleaved. It feels harder and slower, but it trains the skill the interview tests: recognising which pattern to use.
CheckIn an interview nobody tells you "this is a join question". Recognising the pattern is half the marks.

Problem 2.2.2 Your weak spot is "GROUP BY with HAVING". Design deliberate practice for one week.

Step 1Specific goal: write correct GROUP BY / HAVING queries in under 5 minutes.
Step 2Daily 3 problems of rising difficulty; check against a known answer or run on sample data.
Step 3After each miss, write the cause on one line ("used WHERE instead of HAVING").
AnswerAfter 7 days you have about 21 attempts and a list of 3 to 4 repeated errors. Turn each into a flashcard.
Check3 problems x 7 days = 21. If the error list is empty, the problems were too easy: raise the difficulty.

Connection to the real job

Teaching back is what you will do in stand-ups, code reviews and interviews. If you cannot explain a model to a manager, you do not fully own it yet.

Applications

  • Writing a LinkedIn post per week (Chapter 4).
  • Mock interviews (Chapter 7).
  • Mentoring juniors later in your career.
COMMON MISTAKE
Calling "I watched the course" practice. Rule: for every 20 minutes of video, 40 minutes of doing. If you could not do it with the video closed, you have not learned it.

Check yourself

  1. What is interleaving? Answer: mixing different problem types in a session.
  2. What does a gap in teach-back show? Answer: a gap in understanding.
  3. What is deliberate practice? Answer: practising a specific weakness at the edge of ability with fast feedback.

2.3 Avoiding Resource Hopping and Beating Overwhelm

INTUITION
Digging five holes 1 metre deep never gives water. One hole 5 metres deep does. Starting a new course every time the current one gets hard is digging many shallow holes.

The rules

  • One main resource per topic. Choose it in 15 minutes, using a short list of 2 or 3. Finish it.
  • One active project at a time. A project forces you to use what you learn.
  • Finish before starting. Define "finished": all exercises done, one project built, notes in your log.
  • Parking lot. When a new course tempts you, write it in a "later" list with a date. Decide again in 4 weeks.
  • Note system. One notebook or a folder of markdown files (an Obsidian-style vault). Keep a log: "what I learned" and "what confused me". The confused list is gold: it tells you what to study next and what to ask.

Problem 2.3.1 You start 5 courses, each 30 hours, and finish 20% of each. How many hours spent and how many courses completed? What if you finished one?

Step 15 x 30 x 0.20 = 30 hours spent, 0 courses complete.
Step 2Finishing one course: 30 hours, 1 course complete, 1 certificate-worthy skill.
AnswerThe same 30 hours buys either 0 finished courses or 1. Hopping has the same cost and zero finished output.
Check5 x 6 hours (20% of 30) = 30 hours. Same hours, different result.

Problem 2.3.2 Your parking lot has 12 courses. You can finish 1 course every 6 weeks at 1 hour/day. How long does the whole list take, and what do you do?

Step 112 x 6 = 72 weeks, about 16.6 months.
Step 2Most courses overlap in content.
AnswerYou will never finish all. Keep the 2 that fix your biggest current gap, delete the rest, and re-check the list in a month.
Check72 / 4.33 weeks per month = 16.6 months. Correct.

Connection to the real job

Companies pay for finished things: a pipeline running every day, a deployed model. Finishing is a professional habit.

Applications

  • Choosing between courses on Coursera, YouTube, and Zoomcamps.
  • Picking a single book per subject.
  • Setting up your study repo.
COMMON MISTAKE
Searching "best course" for hours. The best course is the one you finish. Spend at most 15 minutes choosing.

Check yourself

  1. What is resource hopping? Answer: changing courses before finishing the current one.
  2. What is a parking lot? Answer: a list of tempting courses you decide on later.
  3. How many projects at a time? Answer: one.

2.4 Energy, Consistency and Wellbeing for a Working Learner

INTUITION
A phone with 5% battery cannot run the best app. If you sleep five hours and then study, the hour is wasted. Charging the battery is part of studying.

The rules that work

  • Fixed slot. Same time daily. After you have eaten, before you are exhausted: perhaps 6:30 am, or 9 pm. Pick the slot you actually keep.
  • Sleep 7+ hours. Memory is stored during sleep. Skipping sleep to study is a bad trade.
  • Pomodoro 25/5. 25 minutes focused, 5 minutes break. Two rounds is an hour.
  • Movement and a weekly rest day. One day with no study, guilt-free.
  • Weekends for projects; weekdays for small steps.
  • 1 hour beats 6 hours occasionally. Seven hours a week as 1 x 7 days is better for memory than 7 hours on Sunday because of spacing.
  • Financial resilience. Keep an emergency fund, avoid loans for courses, stay in your current role until a signed offer.
  • When to seek help. If self-criticism becomes heavy or constant, talk to a trusted person or a counselor. This is part of a sustainable career.

Problem 2.4.1 You have 10 free hours a week. Option A: 10 hours on Sunday. Option B: 1.5 hours a day for 7 days (10.5 h). Compare using spacing.

Step 1In A you meet each topic once a week: one exposure, a gap of 7 days.
Step 2In B you meet topics daily; Anki gaps 1, 3, 7 become possible.
AnswerB gives about the same hours but much better retention, and is less tiring. Use A only for big project days on top of B.
Check1.5 x 7 = 10.5 hours. In plain words: spreading the same hours across the week is the same cost with a better result.

Problem 2.4.2 You miss 3 days in a 30-day month at 1 hour/day. How much did you lose and what do you do?

Step 127 hours studied instead of 30, so you studied 90%.
Step 2Never "make up" 3 hours in one night; just continue tomorrow.
AnswerYou lost 3 hours (10%). A 90% month is excellent. Restart without guilt.
Check27 / 30 = 0.90. You still studied 27 hours, which is more than most people study in a year of good intentions.

The picture

Energy budget (a 24-hour day)
Sleep 7-8h | Job 9h | Commute/chores 4h | Family/rest 3h | STUDY 1h
Study fits only if sleep is protected.

Connection to the real job

Data careers take years. People who stay consistent for 3 years beat people who sprint for 3 months. Burnout stops your career; rest prolongs it.

Applications

  • Planning your daily slot and weekly review.
  • Setting monthly goals (Chapter 14).
  • Talking to your manager about workload.
COMMON MISTAKE
Thinking missed days mean you failed. A streak is a tool, not a judge. Missing one day is nothing; missing two days in a row is the real rule to avoid.

Check yourself

  1. Why is 1 h daily better than 7 h once? Answer: spacing and less fatigue.
  2. How long is a Pomodoro? Answer: 25 minutes work, 5 minutes break.
  3. When should you talk to a counselor? Answer: when self-criticism is heavy or constant.

2.5 A Weekly Learning Plan You Can Actually Run

INTUITION
Learning time is like a monthly salary: if you do not assign it to heads before the month starts, it disappears into small expenses. A written weekly plan is a budget for hours.

The earlier sections gave you methods. This section turns them into a plan you can run on a busy week, with a worked budget, a spacing table and card templates.

Problem 2.5.1 You work 9 to 6, commute 1 hour each way, and want to study. Build a realistic weekly study budget.

Find free timeWeekday evenings: 2 usable hours after dinner on 4 days = 8 hours. Saturday morning: 3 hours. Sunday: 1 hour of review. Total 12 hours.
Apply a reality factorPlans fail at 100%. Take 75%: 12 x 0.75 = 9 hours planned.
Split by methodUsing the 10/30/10/10 style from 2.2: recall 20%, build 60%, interleave 10%, teach back 10%. Hours: 1.8, 5.4, 0.9, 0.9 (sum 9.0).
Check1.8 + 5.4 + 0.9 + 0.9 = 9.0. Yes.
Why it mattersA plan of 9 honest hours beats a plan of 20 imaginary ones. Missing a 20-hour plan hurts your confidence.

Problem 2.5.2 You learn a SQL window-function rule on day 0. Schedule its reviews using gaps of 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days from the previous review, and count the review minutes.

Day numbersReview 1 on day 1; review 2 on day 1 + 3 = 4; review 3 on day 4 + 7 = 11; review 4 on day 11 + 14 = 25; review 5 on day 25 + 30 = 55.
TimeEach review of one card takes about 1 minute, so 5 minutes for the whole life of the card.
20 cards a week20 x 5 = 100 minutes of review for 20 cards over 55 days, about 1.7 hours.
Why it mattersFive minutes per card buys months of memory. Re-reading the whole chapter would cost far more.

COMMON MISTAKE
Making 30 cards in one evening. Wrong: 30 new cards a day for a week creates 210 reviews you cannot finish and then you quit. Right: 3 to 5 new cards a day, only from things you actually got wrong or confused.

The Sunday 10-minute review

  1. Count last week's planned hours against actual hours and write both.
  2. Compute the ratio actual / planned. Below 70% means the plan was too big, so reduce it; do not add guilt.
  3. Choose one topic to build, one to recall, and the one thing to drop.
  4. Put the sessions in your calendar like meetings.

Check yourself

  1. Planned 9 hours, actual 6. Ratio and action? Answer: 6 / 9 = 67%; below 70%, so shrink next week's plan to about 7 hours.
  2. Review days for gaps 1, 3, 7? Answer: Days 1, 4 and 11.
  3. How many new cards on a normal day? Answer: 3 to 5.

Chapter recap

  • Recall beats re-reading. Close the notes, then try.
  • Space your reviews: 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days. Anki does this for you.
  • One idea per card; 3 to 5 new cards a day maximum.
  • Mix problem types (interleaving) because interviews mix them.
  • Explain aloud (teach back) to find gaps.
  • Practise your specific weak spot with fast feedback.
  • One resource, one project, finish before starting something new.
  • Sleep, a fixed slot and a rest day are part of the study plan.
  • Heavy self-criticism is a reason to talk to someone, not to push harder.
  • Budget study hours at about 75% of free time, spread them across recall, build, interleave and teach-back, and review cards at gaps of 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days (section 2.5).

Practice set

  1. (Easy) Learned on day 0, first review is on which day? Answer: Day 1.
  2. (Easy) How many Pomodoro rounds fit in one hour? Answer: 2 (25+5, 25+5).
  3. (Easy) Active recall or re-reading? Which is stronger? Answer: Active recall.
  4. (Medium) You add 4 cards/day for 50 days. How many cards, and lifetime review minutes at 2.5 min each? Answer: 200 cards; 500 minutes.
  5. (Medium) What is wrong with this card: "Explain the entire ML pipeline"? Answer: It is too big. Break into one-idea cards.
  6. (Medium) Find the bug in this plan: "Sunday 8 hours study, nothing else all week." Answer: No spacing, high fatigue, one failed Sunday ruins the week. Spread into daily slots.
  7. (Medium) Scenario: you keep switching courses every two weeks. What do you do? Answer: Pick one for the topic, write down finish criteria, park others for 4 weeks.
  8. (Hard) Interview-style: "How do you keep your skills current while working full time?" Answer: Fixed one-hour slot, one resource and one project at a time, Anki for recall, a weekly post to teach back, monthly review.
  9. (Hard) Interview-style: "Tell me about a time you learned something fast." Answer: Use STAR (Chapter 3): Situation (new tool), Task (deadline), Action (project-first plan, daily recall, asked an expert), Result (delivered in N days).
  10. (Hard) Compute: 3 pomodoro rounds of 25 min is how many focus minutes, and total time with breaks? Answer: 75 focus minutes. With a 5-minute break after each round it is 90 minutes; if you skip the last break it is 85.
  11. (Easy) Free time is 12 hours; you plan at 75%. Planned hours? Answer: 9.
  12. (Medium) First review on day 1, then gaps 3, 7, 14. Day of the fourth review? Answer: 1 + 3 = 4, then 11, then 25. The fourth review is on day 25.
  13. (Interview) How do you keep learning while working full time? Answer: Fixed small slots, a weekly plan at 75% of free time, recall over re-reading, and one project to apply it.
