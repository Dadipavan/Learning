A data model that is accurate but answers the wrong business question is useless. A manager at a bank does not ask for "a classifier with 0.91 AUC"; she asks "how many loans will go bad, and how much will we lose?" This chapter gives you the vocabulary and arithmetic of five big domains in India (banking and insurance, e-commerce, SaaS, telecom, healthcare), a method to learn any new domain in about a month, and worked unit-economics examples computed in Python. By the end you will be able to read a simple business, name its key numbers, and compute whether it makes money.

13.1 Why Domain Knowledge Is a Career Moat

BASIC EXPLANATION
Domain knowledge is understanding of the industry in which you work, such as its goals, terms and rules. It is used to ask the right questions and to make data work useful.
INTUITION
A good mechanic can fix any car. A mechanic who knows rally cars knows which part fails at 200 km/h and what the driver cares about. Same skill, but far more valuable to the rally team. Domain knowledge is knowing what the "driver" cares about.

Words you need

TermMeaning
DomainAn industry area, such as banking or telecom, with its own words, rules and numbers
KPIKey Performance Indicator: a number a team watches to know if things are going well
Unit economicsProfit or loss for one "unit" (one order, one customer, one loan) before scaling up
MoatSomething that protects you from competition; here, a skill hard to copy
RegulationRules set by an authority (in India, for example RBI for banks, IRDAI for insurance, SEBI for markets, TRAI for telecom)

Check each regulator's current rules on its official site. Rules change; this book teaches you where to look, not the latest circular.

How a domain helps you in the job

ItemExampleWhere it appears in the job
Right metricChoosing "loss per loan" over "accuracy"Model evaluation and dashboards
Right featuresDays since last payment for credit riskFeature engineering
Right constraintsCustomer data must stay in India or be consentedArchitecture and privacy
Right story"Saves 12 lakh a month" instead of "AUC improved"Stakeholder presentations (Chapter 5)

Pick one primary and one secondary domain. Any domain can work, but a strong choice connects to a place you could get a job: many GCCs and product firms in India are in finance, retail, telecom and health. Choose by interest, local job market, and any experience you already have (for example, you may have worked on banking or e-commerce applications. If so, you already know their real traffic patterns).

REAL JOB TIP
Think of the applications you have worked on or used. A UPI payment gateway, a retail checkout or a telecom billing system means you already know the system. Say that in interviews: "I know where this system breaks, and now I can analyse why."

13.2 BFSI: Banking, Financial Services and Insurance

BASIC EXPLANATION
BFSI stands for banking, financial services and insurance. These industries manage money and risk, and use data for credit, fraud, pricing and customer analysis.
INTUITION
A bank is a business that rents out money. It pays savers a little, lends to borrowers for more, and survives only if most borrowers repay. So the central data question is: who will not repay, and what will it cost?

Words you need

TermMeaning
BFSIBanking, financial services and insurance
UPIUnified Payments Interface, India's real-time bank-to-bank payment system
NPANon-Performing Asset: a loan on which payment is overdue beyond a limit set by the regulator (check the current RBI rule for the exact period)
GNPA / NNPAGross NPA ratio = NPA / total loans; Net NPA ratio subtracts the money already set aside (provisions)
PDProbability of Default: chance a borrower will fail to repay
LGDLoss Given Default: share of the loan lost if the borrower defaults (after collateral recovered)
EADExposure At Default: amount owed when default happens
KYC / AMLKnow Your Customer / Anti-Money Laundering: identity checks and monitoring for suspicious money flows
Premium, claimIn insurance: the customer pays a premium; the insurer pays a claim when the insured event happens
Loss ratioClaims paid / premium earned
Combined ratio(Claims + expenses) / premium earned; below 100% means an underwriting profit

The credit lifecycle

Apply -> KYC check -> Credit score / underwriting -> Approve & disburse
   -> Repayment monitoring -> Early warning -> Collections -> Recovery / write-off

Walk through it with one loan. A customer applies for a personal loan. The bank checks identity (KYC), looks at credit history (a score from a credit bureau) and income, and decides yes or no (underwriting). It then lends the money, collects monthly payments, watches for late payments (early warning), and if payments stop, tries to collect, then recovers what it can. Data science appears at each box: fraud detection at KYC, a PD model at underwriting, early-warning models during monitoring, collections prioritisation at the end.

Problem 13.2.1 A bank has loans of 5,00,000 (in lakh rupees) and 15,000 of them are NPA. It has set aside 9,000 as provisions. Compute the gross and net NPA ratios.

FormulaGNPA % = NPA / gross loans x 100; NNPA % = (NPA - provisions) / (gross loans - provisions) x 100
Plug inGNPA = 15,000 / 5,00,000; NNPA = (15,000 - 9,000) / (5,00,000 - 9,000)
ComputeGNPA = 0.03 = 3.0%; NNPA = 6,000 / 4,91,000 = 1.22%
AnswerGross NPA is 3.0% and net NPA is about 1.22%.
CheckNet must be smaller than gross because provisions reduce the exposed amount. 1.22 < 3.0. Yes.
Why it mattersIn plain words: 3 of every 100 rupees lent are in trouble, but the bank has already prepared for part of it.

Problem 13.2.2 Expected loss on one loan with PD = 3%, LGD = 45%, EAD = 2,00,000 rupees. For a portfolio of 1,000 such loans, what is the expected loss?

Formulaexpected loss (EL) = PD x LGD x EAD
Plug in0.03 x 0.45 x 2,00,000
Compute0.03 x 0.45 = 0.0135; 0.0135 x 2,00,000 = 2,700
Portfolio1,000 x 2,700 = 27,00,000
AnswerExpected loss is 2,700 rupees per loan and 27 lakh for 1,000 loans.
CheckIf 3% of 1,000 loans default, 30 loans default; each loses 45% of 2,00,000 = 90,000; 30 x 90,000 = 27,00,000. Yes.
Why it mattersThe bank must charge enough interest to cover 2,700 per loan before it earns anything. A better PD model lowers that number.

Problem 13.2.3 An insurer earned 1,000 (in lakh rupees) of premium, paid 650 in claims and spent 280 on expenses. Compute loss ratio and combined ratio.

Computeloss ratio = 650 / 1,000 = 0.65 = 65%; combined = (650 + 280) / 1,000 = 0.93 = 93%
AnswerLoss ratio 65%, combined ratio 93%, so an underwriting profit of 7% of premium.
Check100% - 93% = 7%, and 7% of 1,000 = 70 lakh. Yes.
Why it mattersPricing models try to keep the combined ratio below 100%.

Other BFSI ideas

  • UPI and payments: key numbers are transactions per second, success rate and fraud rate. A success rate of 98% means 2 in 100 payments fail, which at ten million payments a day is 200,000 failures. (Capacity thinking fits here: peak load, timeouts, retries.)
  • Fraud and AML: rare events (very imbalanced classes), where precision and recall trade off with customer annoyance.
  • Regulation: RBI rules on data and outsourcing; the DPDP Act (India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act) for personal data. Read the official texts or reliable summaries for current details.
  • Actuarial basics: insurers estimate expected claims using probability and past data, which is statistics you already know (Math PDF).

Connection to the real job

Common ML use cases: credit scoring, fraud detection, collections prioritisation, customer churn, cross-sell, claims triage, document processing with LLMs.

Applications

  • Data Science: PD models, with explainability needed by regulators.
  • Data Engineering: reconciliation pipelines, daily settlement files, audit trails.
  • Analytics: portfolio dashboards (GNPA by region, product).
  • MLOps: model monitoring and drift for risk models.
COMMON MISTAKE
Optimising accuracy on default prediction. If only 3% default, a model that says "nobody defaults" is 97% accurate and worthless. Use recall, precision and expected loss in rupees.

Check yourself

  1. What is EL = ? Answer: PD x LGD x EAD.
  2. Is a combined ratio of 93% good or bad for an insurer? Answer: good, it means an underwriting profit.
  3. What does KYC do? Answer: verifies customer identity.

13.3 E-commerce and Retail

BASIC EXPLANATION
E-commerce sells products online and retail sells in shops. Data is used for demand, pricing, customer behaviour, delivery and returns.
INTUITION
An online shop is a funnel with a delivery van at the end. Visitors come, a few buy, and each order must pay for its product, its shipping, its returns and the advertising that brought the customer. If one order loses money and you sell a million, you lose a million times more.

Words you need

TermMeaning
GMVGross Merchandise Value: total value of goods ordered
AOVAverage Order Value = GMV / number of orders
Conversion rateOrders / sessions (visits)
CACCustomer Acquisition Cost: marketing spend / new customers won
Contribution marginWhat is left after variable costs of an order (product cost, shipping, payment fees, returns), before fixed costs like office and salaries
Return rateShare of orders returned
MarketplaceA platform where many sellers sell; the platform earns commission and fees

Also know: catalog (the list of products and their attributes), pricing and promotions (discounts), logistics (warehouse, delivery, last mile), search and recommendation (the ML heart of the shop). In India, quick commerce (delivery in minutes from small local warehouses) and open networks such as ONDC are new areas; read current news and company reports because the market changes quickly.

Running example: one shop, one month

Orders 2,000; average order 1,500 rupees; sessions 1,00,000; gross margin 20% of order value; shipping cost 60 per order; 10% of orders returned, and a return costs 120 (reverse shipping and handling); marketing spend 1,00,000; 60% of orders come from new customers.

Problem 13.3.1 Compute GMV, AOV and conversion rate.

FormulaGMV = orders x order value; AOV = GMV / orders; conversion = orders / sessions
ComputeGMV = 2,000 x 1,500 = 30,00,000; AOV = 30,00,000 / 2,000 = 1,500; conversion = 2,000 / 1,00,000 = 0.02
AnswerGMV 30 lakh, AOV 1,500, conversion 2%.
Check1,500 x 2,000 = 3,000,000. Yes.
Why it mattersThe funnel has two levers: more visitors, or more of them buying. A change from 2.0% to 2.2% conversion gives 10% more orders on the same traffic.

Problem 13.3.2 Compute the contribution per order and CAC. Then say if a new customer's first order covers the CAC.

Step 1Gross margin per order = 20% x 1,500 = 300
Step 2Expected return cost = 10% x 120 = 12
Step 3Contribution = 300 - 60 (shipping) - 12 = 228
Step 4New customers = 60% x 2,000 = 1,200; CAC = 1,00,000 / 1,200 = 83.33
AnswerContribution is 228 per order and CAC is about 83. The first order alone covers the CAC, leaving about 145.
Check228 - 83.33 = 144.67. Also total contribution 2,000 x 228 = 4,56,000, minus 1,00,000 marketing = 3,56,000, before fixed costs.
Why it mattersIn plain words: each new customer pays back its advertising on the first order. If shipping were 200, contribution would be 300 - 200 - 12 = 88, and the CAC would barely pay back. Small cost changes decide the business.

Connection to the real job

  • Search and recommendation: ranking products to raise conversion; measured with click-through, add-to-cart and orders per session.
  • Demand forecasting: how much stock for each warehouse.
  • Pricing and promotions: uplift modelling (who buys only because of a discount).
  • Returns prediction and delivery-time estimation.

Applications

  • Data Analytics: funnel dashboards, cohort retention, category performance.
  • Data Engineering: clickstream pipelines, order and inventory tables.
  • ML: ranking, recommenders, demand forecast.
  • GenAI: product-description generation, support chatbots.
COMMON MISTAKE
Looking at GMV and ignoring margin. Growing GMV while contribution per order is negative means growing losses.

Check yourself

  1. AOV = ? Answer: GMV / number of orders.
  2. If conversion goes from 2% to 3% with the same traffic, how many orders increase? Answer: 50% more.
  3. What does contribution margin exclude? Answer: fixed costs.

13.4 SaaS and Product Companies

BASIC EXPLANATION
SaaS (Software as a Service) companies sell software by subscription. Data is used for product usage, retention, churn and revenue.
INTUITION
A SaaS (Software as a Service) company rents software by subscription. It is like a gym: the member pays every month, and the owner cares about how many join, how many stay, and how long it takes to earn back the cost of signing them up.

Words you need

TermMeaning
MRR / ARRMonthly / Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR = MRR x 12)
ARPAAverage Revenue Per Account per month
ChurnShare of customers (or revenue) lost in a period
Gross margin(Revenue - cost to serve) / revenue
LTVLifetime Value: total gross profit from a customer over its life
CAC paybackMonths of gross profit needed to earn back CAC
NRRNet Revenue Retention: revenue from last period's customers now, including upgrades and losses, divided by their revenue then
GRRGross Revenue Retention: like NRR but ignoring upgrades
PLGProduct-Led Growth: users try the product free, then upgrade
ActivationThe moment a new user first gets value (for example creates a first dashboard)

Problem 13.4.1 ARPA is 2,000 per month, gross margin 80%, monthly churn 4%, CAC 24,000. Compute LTV, LTV/CAC and CAC payback.

FormulaLTV = ARPA x gross margin / churn; payback months = CAC / (ARPA x gross margin)
Plug inLTV = 2,000 x 0.80 / 0.04; payback = 24,000 / (2,000 x 0.80)
Computegross profit per month = 1,600; LTV = 1,600 / 0.04 = 40,000; payback = 24,000 / 1,600 = 15; LTV/CAC = 40,000 / 24,000 = 1.67
AnswerLTV is 40,000, payback is 15 months, LTV/CAC is 1.67.
CheckAverage life is 1 / 0.04 = 25 months; 25 x 1,600 = 40,000. Yes.
Why it mattersMany teams look for LTV/CAC around 3 or more, but this is a rule of thumb, not a law. At 1.67 and a 15-month payback this business is weak: either lower CAC, lower churn or raise price.

Problem 13.4.2 A month's MRR starts at 1,00,000. New customers add 12,000; upgrades add 5,000; downgrades remove 2,000; churn removes 4,000. Compute ending MRR, ARR, NRR and GRR.

Step 1Ending MRR = 1,00,000 + 12,000 + 5,000 - 2,000 - 4,000 = 1,11,000
Step 2ARR = 1,11,000 x 12 = 13,32,000
Step 3NRR excludes new customers: (1,00,000 + 5,000 - 2,000 - 4,000) / 1,00,000 = 99,000 / 1,00,000 = 99%
Step 4GRR excludes upgrades: (1,00,000 - 2,000 - 4,000) / 1,00,000 = 94%
AnswerEnding MRR 1,11,000; ARR 13,32,000; NRR 99%; GRR 94%.
CheckNRR minus GRR = 5 points = the upgrades 5,000 / 1,00,000. Yes.
Why it mattersGrowth looks fine (+11%) but NRR below 100% means existing customers shrink; growth depends entirely on new sales.
arpa=2000; churn=0.04; gm=0.80; cac=24000

mrr=start+new+exp-cont-churned
print("MRR end",mrr,"ARR",mrr*12)

Connection to the real job

Product analytics roles compute funnels, retention curves and experiment results. Performance data (page speed) connects directly: slower pages lower activation, and you can measure how much.

Applications

Check yourself

  1. ARR = ? Answer: MRR x 12.
  2. NRR below 100% means what? Answer: existing customers are shrinking in revenue.
  3. What does CAC payback measure? Answer: months of gross profit needed to earn back the CAC.

13.5 Telecom and Healthcare

BASIC EXPLANATION
Telecom companies provide phone and internet services, and healthcare organisations provide medical care. Data is used for customer churn and network quality in telecom, and for patient outcomes and operations in healthcare.
INTUITION
Telecom is a subscription business with a network that must work all day: customers leave if calls drop. Healthcare is a business of care where an error hurts a person and privacy is the law. The same data skills apply, but the cost of a mistake differs.

Telecom words

TermMeaning
ARPUAverage Revenue Per User per period (usually month)
ChurnSubscribers leaving per period
Network KPIsCall drop rate, call setup success, data throughput, latency, tower load
Billing / CDRCharging records; a CDR (Call Detail Record) is one row per call or data session
Prepaid vs postpaidPay before use vs bill after use

Problem 13.5.1 A telecom has revenue of 300 crore over 3 months from 1 crore (10 million) subscribers. Churn is 2% a month. Compute monthly ARPU, expected customer lifetime in months and lifetime revenue.

FormulaARPU = revenue / subscribers / months; lifetime = 1 / monthly churn; lifetime revenue = ARPU x lifetime
Plug inRevenue 3,000,000,000 rupees (300 crore = 3 x 10⁹)
ComputeARPU = 3,000,000,000 / 10,000,000 / 3 = 100; lifetime = 1 / 0.02 = 50 months; lifetime revenue = 100 x 50 = 5,000
AnswerARPU 100 rupees a month, a customer stays 50 months on average and brings about 5,000 rupees.
Check100 rupees x 3 months x 10 million subs = 3 x 10⁹. Yes.
Why it mattersA churn change from 2% to 1.5% lifts lifetime from 50 to 66.7 months, a 33% increase in lifetime revenue.

Problem 13.5.2 A churn model flags 1,000 at-risk customers with ARPU 150. A retention offer costs 30 per customer and saves 20% of them for an extra 3 months of revenue each. Is it worth it?

Plug insaved customers = 1,000 x 0.20 = 200; revenue gain = 200 x 150 x 3 = 90,000; cost = 1,000 x 30 = 30,000
Computenet = 90,000 - 30,000 = 60,000
AnswerNet gain is 60,000 rupees, so the offer is worth it under these assumptions.
CheckBreak-even save rate = cost / (150 x 3 x 1,000) = 30,000 / 4,50,000 = 6.67%. The offer works if it saves more than 6.7% of at-risk customers. At 20% we are well above. Yes.
Why it mattersA churn model is only valuable when linked to an action and its cost.

Healthcare words

TermMeaning
EHR / EMRElectronic Health/Medical Record: a patient's digital chart
Clinical dataDiagnoses, lab results, prescriptions, images
OutcomeThe result for the patient (recovered, readmitted)
SensitivityShare of sick patients a test correctly flags
SpecificityShare of healthy people a test correctly clears

Problem 13.5.3 A screening test for a condition with prevalence 1% has sensitivity 90% and specificity 95%. Among 100,000 people, what share of positive results are true positives?

FormulaPPV = TP / (TP + FP)
Step 1Sick people = 1% x 1,00,000 = 1,000; healthy = 99,000
Step 2TP = 0.90 x 1,000 = 900; FP = (1 - 0.95) x 99,000 = 4,950
Step 3PPV = 900 / (900 + 4,950) = 900 / 5,850 = 0.1538
AnswerOnly about 15.4% of positive results are real. Most positives are false alarms because the condition is rare.
CheckPython gave 0.1538. Also total positives = 5,850 of 1,00,000 = 5.85%.
Why it mattersA doctor needs a second test after a positive. Reporting only "90% sensitive" would mislead patients. This is the same base-rate effect as in fraud detection (Chapter 7, stats).

Privacy and ethics

Health data is highly sensitive. Use consent, minimise the data you keep, remove identifiers, control access, and follow the law (in India the DPDP Act; other countries have HIPAA or GDPR). Do not use real patient data in public projects. Use public or synthetic datasets only.

Applications

  • Telecom: churn, network anomaly detection, tower planning, fraud (SIM-box, spam calls).
  • Healthcare: medical imaging, readmission prediction, claims, appointment no-show, clinical notes with NLP (privacy rules apply).
  • Pharma: trial data analysis, supply chain, adverse-event monitoring.
COMMON MISTAKE
Judging a rare-event test by accuracy or sensitivity alone. Always compute PPV with the real prevalence.

Check yourself

  1. ARPU stands for? Answer: Average Revenue Per User.
  2. If PPV is low, what does a positive result mean? Answer: many positives are false alarms.
  3. Why avoid real patient data in public projects? Answer: privacy and legal risk.

Problem 13.6.1 A warehouse delivers 9,200 of 10,000 parcels on time. A new routing change leads to 9,500 on time. What are the on-time rates and the improvement in percentage points and in late parcels?

Computebefore 92%; after 95%; improvement 3 percentage points; late parcels fall from 800 to 500, which is 37.5% fewer

Problem 13.6.2 Gaming: 10,000 players install. 4,000 return on day 1, 1,500 on day 7, 600 on day 30. Compute retention each day.

ComputeD1 = 4,000 / 10,000 = 40%; D7 = 1,500 / 10,000 = 15%; D30 = 600 / 10,000 = 6%

CheckRetention is non-increasing over days (40 > 15 > 6). Yes.

13.7 How to Learn a Domain Fast

BASIC EXPLANATION
Learning a domain fast means studying its main terms, metrics, processes and data sources in a planned way. It is used to become useful in a new industry quickly.
INTUITION
Learning a domain is like learning a city. You first memorise the main roads (vocabulary), then walk a few streets (a project), then ask locals (experts). You do not need to know every lane.

The four-week plan (1 hour a day)

WeekDoOutput
1Read two annual reports or investor presentations; list unknown wordsGlossary of 50 terms
2Listen to or read two earnings-call transcripts; note the numbers management talks about mostOne page of 10 KPIs and their formulas
3Talk to two people in the domain; ask what data problems cost them moneyNotes with 3 real problems
4Build one small project on public data (UPI statistics, a retail dataset, a telecom churn dataset) with a business question and a rupee estimateA repo with a README (Chapter 11)

Also: shadow a business meeting if you can; read industry reports; follow two newsletters; use Lean Analytics, Inspired by Marty Cagan and Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments by Kohavi and others for product thinking.

The glossary habit

Write each term as: Term | plain meaning | formula (if any) | where it shows up in data. Review with spaced repetition (Chapter 2).

Questions to ask a domain expert

  1. What number do you check first every morning?
  2. What decision costs the most when it is wrong?
  3. What data do you wish you had?
  4. What happened the last time something went badly wrong?

Problem 13.7.1 You learn 50 terms in 4 weeks, with the plan "10 new terms per day, 5 days". Is that realistic? What daily pace is better?

Compute10 x 5 = 50 terms in 5 days, but memory fades; with spaced review, 5 new terms a day for 10 days (50 terms) leaves time to review.
Answer50 terms in 4 weeks is 2.5 new terms per working day (20 days), a comfortable pace with review.
Check50 / 20 = 2.5. Yes.
Why it mattersSlow with review beats fast without it.

Problem 13.7.2 Write a domain project question with a rupee estimate. Example: "A bank sends 10,000 reminders a month. If a model targets the 20% likely to miss payment, how much does it save?" Suppose each message costs 2 rupees and recovers 150 rupees on 25% of targeted messages.

Step 1Send to 2,000 (20% of 10,000); cost = 2,000 x 2 = 4,000
Step 2Recoveries = 2,000 x 0.25 = 500; recovered value = 500 x 150 = 75,000
Step 3Net = 75,000 - 4,000 = 71,000
AnswerUnder the assumptions, net benefit is 71,000 rupees a month. State assumptions clearly, since the 25% and 150 are guesses to be tested.
CheckCost per recovery = 4,000 / 500 = 8 rupees for 150 recovered. Yes.

Connection to the real job

In interviews, show domain curiosity: "I read your annual report; I noticed cost-to-serve is falling. How does the data team contribute to that?"

13.8 Worked Exercises by Domain

BASIC EXPLANATION
A worked exercise applies domain knowledge to a realistic question, for example choosing metrics for a bank or a shop. It is used to practise thinking in the language of the industry.
INTUITION
Domain knowledge is a language with a few dozen key words. Once you can compute five metrics of a domain by hand, you can follow its meetings. These exercises give you that: one small calculation for each domain. All numbers are made-up practice figures.

Problem 13.8.1 BFSI. A bank has loans of 2,000 crore, gross NPA of 80 crore and provisions of 50 crore. Find gross and net NPA ratios.

Formulasgross = NPA / loans; net = (NPA - provisions) / (loans - provisions)
Computegross = 80 / 2,000 = 0.04; net = (80 - 50) / (2,000 - 50) = 30 / 1,950 = 0.01538
AnswerGross NPA 4.0%; net NPA 1.54%.
CheckProvisions lower the burden, so net must be below gross. Yes (1.54 < 4).
Why it mattersA data analyst at a bank reports both and explains why they differ.

Problem 13.8.2 BFSI. A loan has PD 2%, LGD 45% and exposure 10,00,000 rupees. Expected loss?

Formulaexpected loss = PD x LGD x EAD
Compute0.02 x 0.45 x 10,00,000 = 0.009 x 10,00,000 = 9,000
Answer9,000 rupees per loan; for 1,000 identical loans, 90 lakh.
Check2% of loans default (20 of 1,000); each loses 45% of 10 lakh = 4.5 lakh; 20 x 4.5 lakh = 90 lakh. Same.

Problem 13.8.3 E-commerce. 50,000 orders at average order value 800 rupees; 12% of order value is returned. Net revenue?

Gross50,000 x 800 = 4,00,00,000 (4 crore)
Returns12% of 4 crore = 48,00,000
Net4,00,00,000 - 48,00,000 = 3,52,00,000
Answer3.52 crore.
Check0.88 x 4 crore = 3.52 crore. Yes.

Problem 13.8.4 SaaS. Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) is 10,00,000; 3% of it churns each month; new MRR of 50,000 is added. MRR next month? Also, ARPU 2,000 per month and churn 4% per month with CAC 15,000: LTV/CAC?

MRR10,00,000 x 0.97 = 9,70,000; plus 50,000 = 10,20,000 (a 2% rise)
LTVARPU / churn = 2,000 / 0.04 = 50,000
Ratio50,000 / 15,000 = 3.33
AnswerMRR 10.2 lakh; LTV/CAC about 3.3.
CheckA common rule of thumb is a ratio near 3 or above, but it depends on margins and payback time; treat it as a guide, not a law.

Problem 13.8.5 Telecom. 20 lakh subscribers, monthly churn 2%, ARPU 150 rupees. Monthly revenue lost to churn, and the share still subscribed after 12 months of 2% churn?

Leavers20,00,000 x 0.02 = 40,000
Revenue lost40,000 x 150 = 60,00,000 per month (60 lakh)
Survival0.98^12 = 0.785
Answer60 lakh a month; about 78.5% remain after a year, so 21.5% are lost.
Check12 x 2% = 24% would be wrong because each month's base shrinks. 21.5% < 24%. Yes.

Problem 13.8.6 Healthcare. A hospital had 45 readmissions within 30 days among 300 discharges. After a follow-up call program, 36 among 320. Change?

Before45 / 300 = 15.0%
After36 / 320 = 11.25%
AnswerDown 3.75 percentage points, a relative fall of 25%.
Check3.75 / 15 = 0.25. Yes.
CaveatTwo single periods do not prove the program caused the fall; check patient mix and season.
COMMON MISTAKE
Quoting a domain metric without its definition. Wrong: "Churn is 2%." Right: "Monthly logo churn is 2%: customers who cancelled in the month divided by customers at the start of it." Teams argue for weeks over two different definitions.

Make your own domain exercise

  1. Choose your target domain and list its five most-used metrics.
  2. For each, write the formula, one made-up example and the check, as above.
  3. Ask one person from that domain whether the definitions match their company. Fix what differs.

Check yourself

  1. Gross NPA 5 on loans 100: ratio? Answer: 5%.
  2. Why is 12 x 2% = 24% wrong for yearly churn? Answer: The base shrinks each month; compounding gives 21.5% (1 - 0.98^12).
  3. Net revenue with 10% returns on 100 crore? Answer: 90 crore.

Chapter recap

  • Pick one primary domain by interest, jobs nearby and prior knowledge; use a score, not a mood.
  • BFSI: NPA ratios, expected loss = PD x LGD x EAD, combined ratio; accuracy is a trap with rare defaults.
  • E-commerce: GMV, AOV, conversion, CAC and contribution margin per order; growth with negative contribution is growing loss.
  • SaaS: MRR/ARR, churn, LTV, CAC payback, NRR vs GRR; NRR below 100% means existing customers shrink.
  • Telecom: ARPU and churn give lifetime value; a churn model needs an action with a cost.
  • Healthcare: PPV depends on prevalence; privacy and consent are legal duties.
  • Distinguish percent from percentage points; state assumptions with every rupee estimate.
  • Learn a domain in four weeks: glossary, KPIs, experts, one small project.
  • Practise one hand-calculated metric per domain: NPA ratios and expected loss, net revenue after returns, MRR and LTV/CAC, compounded churn, readmission rate (section 13.8).

Practice set

  1. (Easy) Loans 2,000, NPA 60. GNPA %? Answer: 60 / 2,000 = 3%.
  2. (Easy) 500 orders from 20,000 sessions. Conversion? Answer: 2.5%.
  3. (Easy) MRR 50,000. ARR? Answer: 6,00,000.
  4. (Medium) PD 2%, LGD 40%, EAD 1,00,000. EL? Answer: 0.02 x 0.4 x 1,00,000 = 800.
  5. (Medium) ARPA 1,000, gross margin 70%, churn 5%. LTV? Answer: 700 / 0.05 = 14,000.
  6. (Medium) Find the bug: a student computes lifetime revenue for ARPU 150 and churn 2% with print(round(150/(1000*0.02),1)) and gets 7.5. Answer: The formula is ARPU / churn = 150 / 0.02 = 7,500. The extra 1000* in the code is the bug (it printed 7.5 when run).
  7. (Medium) Find the bug: "Sales rose from 10% to 12%, a 2% rise." Answer: It is 2 percentage points, or a 20% relative rise.
  8. (Medium) Order value 1,000, margin 25%, shipping 50, return rate 8% at cost 100 each. Contribution? Answer: 250 - 50 - 8 = 192.
  9. (Hard) Start MRR 2,00,000; new 20,000; upgrades 10,000; downgrades 4,000; churn 12,000. NRR and GRR? Answer: NRR = (2,00,000 + 10,000 - 4,000 - 12,000) / 2,00,000 = 1,94,000 / 2,00,000 = 97%. GRR = (2,00,000 - 16,000) / 2,00,000 = 92%.
  10. (Hard) Prevalence 2%, sensitivity 95%, specificity 90%. PPV for 10,000 people? Answer: Sick 200, TP 190; healthy 9,800, FP 980; PPV = 190 / 1,170 = 16.2%.
  11. (Interview) "A bank wants to reduce loan defaults. What would you ask first?" Answer: What counts as default, the current default rate and loss per default, what actions are possible (lower limit, early call), how much data history exists, and regulatory limits on features and explainability.
  12. (Interview) "Our e-commerce conversion fell from 2.5% to 2.2%. How do you investigate?" Answer: Split by channel, device, new vs returning, page, and time (an issue tree, Chapter 6); check site speed and errors (your performance skills), stock-outs, price changes and tracking bugs before blaming demand.
  13. (Medium) Gross NPA 60 on loans 1,500. Ratio? Answer: 60 / 1,500 = 4%.
  14. (Medium) Monthly churn 3%. Share remaining after 6 months? Answer: 0.97^6 = 0.833, so about 83.3%.
  15. (Hard) LTV is ARPU / churn. ARPU 1,000, churn 5%, CAC 8,000. Is LTV/CAC at least 3? Answer: LTV = 20,000; ratio 2.5, below 3 by the rule of thumb.
