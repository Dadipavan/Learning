CHAPTER 4

Written Communication

Most of your work life is written: emails, tickets, docs, messages. Good writing is how people trust you when you are not in the room.

In a data job, your SQL or model may be perfect and still be ignored if your message is confusing. Remote and hybrid teams, and global companies with teams in many time zones, depend on writing. This chapter shows you how to write an email that gets a reply, a README that lets a stranger run your project, a design record that explains why you chose something, a Slack question that gets a fast answer, and a weekly post that builds your name. By the end you will have four templates you can copy and use this week.

4.1 Emails and Executive Summaries

INTUITION
Your manager reads 80 emails a day. An email is like a lift ride: you have about 10 seconds. If the ask is not in the first line, the ride is over. So write like a newspaper: the headline first, details after.

The rules

  • Subject line contains the ask. Bad: "Query". Good: "Decision needed by Fri: use March or April data for churn report?"
  • Lead with the conclusion. First sentence = the answer or the request.
  • Use bullets for details (3 to 5 bullets).
  • End with clear next steps: who does what by when.
  • Keep it short. Under 150 words for most work emails.

An executive summary is a short top section of a report for busy leaders. Format: one line with the headline result and a number, then 3 bullets of evidence, then a recommendation, then risks.

Words you need

WordMeaning
StakeholderA person who cares about or depends on your work
AskThe thing you want the reader to do
BLUF"Bottom line up front": conclusion first
Executive summaryOne page, numbers first

Problem 4.1.1 Rewrite this email. Subject: "Hi". Body: "I was checking the pipeline and there were some things, the load took long, and I think maybe we need more memory, let me know your thoughts."

Step 1Make the subject carry the ask: "Approval needed: raise pipeline memory from 8 GB to 16 GB".
Step 2Lead with the conclusion: "I recommend doubling the pipeline memory to 16 GB."
Step 3Add evidence in bullets (use true numbers; these are an example): "Nightly load now takes 3 h 10 min, past the 2 h target. Memory is at 95% for the last 40 minutes. A test with 16 GB finished in 1 h 20 min."
Step 4Next steps: "Please approve by Friday. I will make the change Saturday night."
AnswerA subject, a one-line recommendation, 3 evidence bullets and an approval request with dates, about 70 words.
CheckTime saved: 3 h 10 min = 190 min and 1 h 20 min = 80 min, so (190 - 80) / 190 = 57.9% faster. The email can be understood in 10 seconds.
Why it mattersThe reader can approve without a meeting.

Problem 4.1.2 Write a one-line headline for an executive summary. Facts: in the south region the price rose from 100 to 110 rupees, and units sold fell by 12%. What happened to revenue?

Formularevenue = price x units
Plug inprice change factor = 110 / 100 = 1.10; units change factor = 1 - 0.12 = 0.88
Computerevenue factor = 1.10 x 0.88 = 0.968, so revenue changed by 0.968 - 1 = -0.032, which is -3.2%
Answer"In the south region, a 10% price rise cut units by 12%, so revenue fell about 3.2%."
CheckTake 1,000 units at 100 rupees = 100,000. After the change: 880 units x 110 = 96,800. (100,000 - 96,800) / 100,000 = 3.2%. Matches.
Why it mattersIn plain words: the price rise did not pay for itself. Writing numbers forces you to calculate them, and a summary with wrong numbers destroys trust.

The picture

Read it from top to bottom: it is the order of lines in the email.

Subject: [ASK + DEADLINE]
Line 1:  Conclusion / request
Bullets: evidence (3-5)
Last:    who does what by when

Connection to the real job

Weekly status mails, incident reports (what broke, impact, cause, fix, prevention) and stakeholder updates all follow this shape. A monthly sales report is already an executive summary if you put the numbers first.

Applications

  • Analyst: weekly KPI email.
  • Data Engineer: incident summary after a pipeline failure.
  • Data Scientist / ML: model result email with accuracy and a clear recommendation.
COMMON MISTAKE
A long email with the ask hidden in paragraph four. If the reader must search for the ask, they will reply "what do you need?" and you lose a day.

Check yourself

  1. Where does the ask go? Answer: in the subject line and the first sentence.
  2. What is an executive summary? Answer: a one-page summary with numbers first.
  3. What ends a good email? Answer: clear next steps with owner and date.

4.2 Documentation: README, Design Docs, ADRs, Runbooks

INTUITION
Imagine you go on leave for a month. A new person must run your pipeline at 2 am when it breaks. Documentation is the note you leave on the kitchen table. If it is clear, they cook without calling you.

Words you need

DocumentPurposeReader
READMEHow to understand and run a projectNew user or hiring manager
Design docWhat we will build and why, before buildingTeam and reviewers
ADR (Architecture Decision Record)One decision, its reasons and alternativesFuture team members
RunbookStep-by-step fix for a known problemOn-call engineer at 2 am
Data dictionaryEach column's meaning, type and sourceAnalysts and engineers

README template

# Project name
One sentence: what problem it solves.

## Result
One headline number or screenshot.

## How to run
1. git clone ...
2. pip install -r requirements.txt
3. python main.py

## How it works
Short diagram + 5 bullets.

## Data
Source, size, license.

## Decisions and limits
What I chose and why; what does not work yet.

## Next steps

ADR template

# ADR 003: Use Postgres for the orders store
Status: Accepted (2026-11-07)
Context: We need transactions and queries by customer; 5 million rows/year.
Decision: Use Postgres.
Alternatives considered: MongoDB (flexible, but we need joins), DynamoDB (cost model unclear).
Consequences: Plan for read replicas after 50 million rows. Easy for the team (SQL skills).

An ADR is short (half a page). Its value is the reasons: in two years nobody remembers why. Write what was decided, when, and what you would revisit.

Design doc outline

Problem, goals and non-goals, proposed design (diagram), alternatives, risks, rollout plan, open questions. Share it before building. Reviewers find problems when they are cheap to fix.

Runbook example

Alert: nightly_orders_load failed
1. Open Airflow, find the failed task, read the last 50 log lines.
2. If error is "connection timeout": re-run the task once.
3. If it fails again: check the source DB status page; message #data-oncall.
4. If row count < 90% of yesterday: do NOT publish; notify finance.
Owner: Data Platform team. Last tested: 2026-10-15.

Problem 4.2.1 Write a one-sentence README opening for a project that loads a school's exam results into a database and charts the average mark by class.

Step 1State the problem: engineers cannot compare p95 across releases.
Step 2State the solution and tools: a pipeline in Python and SQLite with a chart per release.
Answer"This project loads exam result files into a database and draws the average mark for every class, so a drop can be seen in seconds."
CheckDoes it say what, for whom, and why? What: loads and draws. Who: engineers (implied). Why: find a regression. It is one sentence of 25 words.
Why it mattersHiring managers read the first line only. Make it count.

Problem 4.2.2 Write the key line of an ADR for choosing batch (nightly) over streaming for a daily sales report.

Step 1Facts: the report is read once at 9 am; data is available at 1 am; streaming needs more infrastructure.
Step 2Decision: batch.
Answer"Decision: use a nightly batch job at 2 am, because the report is read once a day at 9 am and streaming adds cost and complexity without a user benefit. Revisit if the business needs hourly numbers."
CheckThe ADR includes the reason and the condition to revisit. Both matter. In plain words: a decision without a reason cannot be challenged or improved.

The picture

Idea -> Design doc (before) -> Build -> README (how to run) -> Runbook (how to fix)
              |
            ADRs record each important decision along the way

Read left to right: documents are written at different moments for different readers.

Connection to the real job

Interview take-homes and portfolio projects are judged on README quality. In companies, a good runbook reduces 2 am calls.

Applications

  • Data Engineering: data dictionary for each table, runbook per pipeline.
  • ML: model card (what the model does, data, metrics, limits).
  • Analytics: a page of metric definitions.
COMMON MISTAKE
Writing docs once and never updating them. Add the date and owner, and update when you change the code.

Check yourself

  1. What does an ADR record? Answer: one decision with reasons and alternatives.
  2. Who reads a runbook? Answer: the on-call engineer fixing a known issue.
  3. What is the first line of a README? Answer: one sentence on the problem solved.

4.3 Slack Etiquette and Asking Good Questions

INTUITION
A doctor cannot help if you say "I feel bad." She asks: where, since when, what did you try? A good question gives the answerer everything needed, so they can answer in one reply.

Slack and Teams etiquette

  • Put the question in the first message; do not send just "Hi" and wait.
  • Use threads, so the channel stays readable.
  • Use the right channel; do not send private messages for questions others also have.
  • Respect time zones and working hours; do not expect an instant answer.
  • Say thanks and post the solution when you solve it. The next person will find it.
  • Mark urgency honestly. Not everything is urgent.

The four-part question

  1. What I am trying to do (goal).
  2. What I tried (steps, commands).
  3. Expected vs actual (with the exact error message).
  4. Minimal example (the smallest code or data that shows the problem).

Good question example

"Hi team, I am loading a CSV of 2 million rows into Postgres with Python. Goal: finish in under 10 minutes. I tried pandas.to_sql with default settings; it took 47 minutes and used 6 GB RAM. I also tried chunksize=50000: 21 minutes. A minimal example and the schema are in the thread. Any suggestion on bulk loading?"

Problem 4.3.1 Rewrite this message: "My code is not working, please help."

Step 1Goal: "I am joining orders and customers in pandas."
Step 2Tried: "I used orders.merge(customers, on='cust_id')."
Step 3Expected vs actual: "Expected 1,000 rows, got 1,200."
Step 4Find the clue and add a minimal example: "customers.cust_id has duplicate ids; here are 5 sample rows from each table."
Answer"I am joining orders (1,000 rows) with customers using orders.merge(customers, on='cust_id'). I expected 1,000 rows but got 1,200. I checked and 200 orders match a duplicated cust_id. Here are 5 sample rows from each table. Should I drop duplicates first, or is there a better key?"
CheckThe numbers explain the bug: 1,000 + 200 extra matches = 1,200. A reader can answer at once: deduplicate customers.
Why it mattersWriting the question often answers it. Many engineers say "while writing the question I found the bug."

Problem 4.3.2 A colleague is in a UTC team. It is 5 pm IST for you. What time is it in UTC, and when might a reply come?

FormulaUTC = IST - 5 h 30 min (IST is the Indian time zone; it is 5 h 30 min ahead of UTC)
Plug in17:00 - 5:30
Compute11:30
AnswerIt is 11:30 UTC. A UTC colleague's day ends around 17:00 UTC, which is 22:30 IST, so a reply could come late at night or the next morning your time. Send a complete question so they can answer in one go.
Check11:30 + 5:30 = 17:00 IST. Correct.
Why it mattersA half-written question across time zones costs a full day per round trip.

The picture

Bad:   "Help"  -> reply: "What is the problem?"  -> 1 day lost
Good:  goal + tried + expected/actual + example -> reply: the answer

Connection to the real job

Senior people judge junior people by their questions. A clean question shows you tried first and respect their time. It is also how you get answers from open-source communities (Chapter 14).

Applications

  • Team chat and code reviews.
  • Stack Overflow and GitHub issues.
  • AI assistants: the same four parts give much better AI answers.
COMMON MISTAKE
Pasting a screenshot of an error instead of the text. Text can be searched and copied. Paste the full error as text in a code block.

Check yourself

  1. What are the four parts of a good question? Answer: goal, tried, expected vs actual, minimal example.
  2. Why use threads? Answer: they keep the channel readable.
  3. Should you paste errors as screenshots? Answer: no, use text.

4.4 Learning in Public: Weekly Posts and Tools

INTUITION
A shop with a closed door gets no customers, even if the shop is excellent. Posting what you learn opens the door a little. One short post a week for a year makes 52 posts. Hiring managers, referrers and mentors find you through these.

What to post

  • TIL ("Today I learned"): 80 to 150 words, one small lesson.
  • Project write-up: problem, approach, result with number, mistakes.
  • Paper summary (Chapter 10).
  • Comparison with a benchmark: for example, two ways to load data, with timings.

A simple template for a weekly post

Title: What I learned: window functions in 20 minutes
1 line: the problem I had
3-5 lines: what I did (with a small code snippet or number)
1 line: the mistake I made
1 line: what I will try next

Tools

  • Grammarly or LanguageTool (free tiers exist) to catch grammar errors. Learn from the corrections.
  • Read your post aloud once. If you run out of breath, the sentence is too long.
  • Keep sentences under 20 words. One idea per sentence.

Ethics of posting

Never post confidential company data, client names, internal numbers or screenshots from work. Use public or simulated data. Check your employment contract if unsure.

Problem 4.4.1 You plan 52 short posts (45 minutes each) and 24 blog posts (3 hours each) a year. How many hours is that, and is it realistic?

Formulatime = posts x hours per post + blogs x hours per blog
Plug in52 x 0.75 + 24 x 3
Compute52 x 0.75 = 39 hours; 24 x 3 = 72 hours; total = 111 hours
AnswerAbout 111 hours a year, or 2.1 hours a week. That is too heavy if you only study 7 hours a week.
Check111 / 52 = 2.13. With 12 blogs instead (36 hours) the total is 75 hours, 1.4 hours a week. The skill map says 2 posts a month is plenty; here 12 blogs a year is even lighter, and you can still do it.
Why it mattersPlan the writing load honestly or you will drop it after 3 weeks.

Problem 4.4.2 Make this sentence shorter: "In this particular post I would like to try to explain in some detail about how it is that joins work."

Step 1Remove filler: "In this particular post", "I would like to try to", "in some detail", "how it is that".
Step 2Keep subject, verb, object.
Answer"This post explains how joins work." That is 6 words instead of 21.
CheckThe original has 21 words (count them). 21 - 6 = 15 words saved, about 71% shorter, with the same meaning.

The picture

Learn something -> write 120 words -> post on Tuesday -> comments -> next idea
        ^                                                         |
        +---------------------------------------------------------+

Connection to the real job

Chapter 11 turns your posts into a portfolio. Recruiters and referrers read your last 5 posts to judge if you are real.

Applications

  • LinkedIn posts, dev.to, Hashnode, Medium, a personal blog.
  • Internal company wiki pages (visibility for internal transfers).
COMMON MISTAKE
Waiting for a perfect, long post. Short and honest beats long and never published. Post what you learned this week.

Check yourself

  1. What is a TIL post? Answer: a short post on one thing you learned today.
  2. Name a tool for grammar. Answer: Grammarly or LanguageTool.
  3. What should you never post? Answer: confidential company data.

4.5 Email and Slack Rewrite Workshop

INTUITION
A bad email is like a parcel with no address label: all the content may be fine, but nobody knows where it goes or what to do with it. A good email has the address on top: the ask.

Here is a small workshop. You see a weak message, rewrite it, and score it. Then you keep the templates.

Problem 4.5.1 Rewrite this email. Subject: "Hi". Body: "Hope you are doing well. I was looking at the data and there are some issues which may need to be looked at when you have time. Let me know."

Find the askYou need the manager to decide whether to delay Friday's report.
SubjectDecision needed by Wed: delay Friday sales report?
Line 1"Recommendation: delay the report by one day, because 4% of orders are duplicated."
Bullets- 750 of 18,400 orders (4.1%) are duplicates.\n- Fix and recheck take 1 day.\n- Sending today would overstate sales by about 4%.
Next step"Please reply by Wednesday noon. If I hear nothing I will send the corrected report Friday evening."
Check750 / 18,400 = 0.0408, so 4.1%. Yes.

Three templates to keep

STATUS UPDATE (weekly)
Subject: [Project] week 12: on track / at risk
Done: ... Next: ... Risks: ... Need from you: ...

ESCALATION (polite)
Subject: Blocked: access to [table], needed by [date]
I asked on [date]. Without it, [impact in days]. Could you help me reach [owner]?

ASKING A GOOD QUESTION (Slack)
Goal: ... Tried: ... Error/evidence: ... Question: one sentence

Problem 4.5.2 Score the rewritten email with a 4-part check: Subject carries the ask, line 1 is the conclusion, evidence is in bullets, next step has a date.

Subject1 point: contains the decision and the deadline.
Line 11 point: recommendation first.
Bullets1 point: three short facts with numbers.
Next step1 point: a day and a default action.
Total4 out of 4. The original scored 0 out of 4.
COMMON MISTAKE
Long politeness before the ask. Wrong: three lines of greeting, then a vague "let me know". Right: one greeting, then the conclusion in the first line. Being direct is not rude; it saves the reader's time.

Check yourself

  1. Where does the ask go? Answer: In the subject and the first line.
  2. What does an escalation email state? Answer: What you asked, when, the impact in days, and what help you want.
  3. 750 of 18,400 as a percent? Answer: About 4.1%.

Chapter recap

  • Put the ask in the email subject and the conclusion in the first line.
  • A one-page executive summary has numbers first.
  • READMEs say what, how to run, result and limits.
  • ADRs record decisions with reasons; runbooks help the on-call person.
  • Ask questions with goal, tried, expected vs actual, minimal example.
  • Use threads, channels and time-zone respect in chat.
  • Post one short thing a week; keep sentences under 20 words.
  • Never share confidential data.
  • Rewrite weak messages with the ask in the subject, the conclusion in line 1, evidence in bullets and a dated next step (section 4.5).

Practice set

  1. (Easy) What is BLUF? Answer: Bottom line up front: put the conclusion first.
  2. (Easy) Name two items in a README. Answer: How to run, result, data, limits (any two).
  3. (Easy) 15:00 IST is what time in UTC? Answer: 09:30.
  4. (Medium) Rewrite the subject "Update" for a request to approve 2 extra days. Answer: "Approval needed by Wed: extend data migration by 2 days".
  5. (Medium) What is wrong with "Hi, are you there?" in Slack? Answer: It forces a wait. Send the full question in the first message.
  6. (Medium) What is the result? Price 200 rises 5% and units fall 4%. Revenue change? Answer: 1.05 x 0.96 = 1.008, so revenue rises 0.8%.
  7. (Medium) Find the bug: an ADR says only "We chose Kafka." Answer: No reasons, no alternatives. Add context, why, alternatives, consequences.
  8. (Hard) Interview: "How do you document a data pipeline?" Answer: README (purpose, run steps), data dictionary, ADRs for key choices, runbook for failures, with owners and dates; keep it near the code.
  9. (Hard) Interview: "How do you ask for help when stuck?" Answer: I define the goal, list what I tried with timings, give expected vs actual and a minimal example, then ask in the team thread after 30 to 60 minutes of trying.
  10. (Hard) Scenario: you wrote a post using real company numbers. What do you do? Answer: Remove it, replace the numbers with simulated or public data, and check the company policy before posting again.
  11. (Easy) What is wrong with the subject "Hi"? Answer: It carries no ask, so the reader cannot prioritise it.
  12. (Medium) 90 of 1,500 rows are bad. Percent? Answer: 6%.
  13. (Interview) How do you write to a senior who has not replied for 3 days? Answer: A short follow-up with the original ask, the impact of waiting, and a proposed default.
