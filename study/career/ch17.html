Good technical skill gets you the interview. Clear English helps you win it. In English, the verb changes its form to show when something happens, and also how it relates to other times. These forms are called tenses. Many learners know the words but mix the tenses, and this makes a good answer sound confusing. This chapter explains all 12 tenses in simple English. For each one you get a plain definition, a picture to remember, the formula, examples from data and AI work, signal words and common mistakes. Then you learn how to choose a tense, how to use tenses in interviews, stand-ups and emails, and how to fix the most common errors. Chapter 3 (Spoken English) and Chapter 4 (Written Communication) show where to use this in daily work.

17.1 What a Tense Is

BASIC EXPLANATION
A tense is the form of a verb that tells us when something happens (past, present or future) and what kind of action it is (a simple fact, an action in progress, a finished result, or a long action up to now).
INTUITION
Think of a verb as a camera. The tense tells the listener where the camera points on the timeline and how wide the picture is. "I work" is a normal-day photo. "I am working" is a live video right now. "I have worked" is a photo of the result that is still important today.

Two questions every tense answers

  • When? Past, present or future.
  • How? Simple (a fact or habit), continuous (in progress), perfect (finished, linked to another time), or perfect continuous (in progress for some time, up to a point).

3 times x 4 types = 12 tenses. You do not need to learn 12 separate things. You learn 3 time zones and 4 shapes, and the shapes repeat in each zone.

Auxiliary (helper) verbs

Most tenses need a helper verb before the main verb. The helpers are do/does/did, am/is/are/was/were, have/has/had and will. The helper carries the tense. The main verb carries the meaning.

WordMeaning
TenseVerb form that shows time and type of action
Auxiliary verbHelper verb: do, be, have, will
Base verbThe plain verb: work, build, train
V1, V2, V3Base form, past form (worked, built), past participle (worked, built, done, gone)
Regular verbPast and V3 end in -ed: work, worked, worked
Irregular verbPast and V3 change: go, went, gone; build, built, built
Time markerA word like yesterday, now, since, tomorrow
COMMON MISTAKE
Learning rules only in your head. Tenses become natural only when you say and write sentences about your own work. Use your own projects as examples.

17.2 The Three Time Zones

BASIC EXPLANATION
English divides time into three zones: past (before now), present (now and around now), and future (after now). Every tense belongs to one of these zones.
INTUITION
Draw a line on paper. Mark a point called NOW. Left of the point is the past, the point and the area around it is the present, and right of the point is the future. A tense is just a place on this line plus a shape.
ZoneMeaningSimple example
PastBefore nowI trained the model yesterday.
PresentNow, or true in generalI train models every week.
FutureAfter nowI will train the model tomorrow.

The 4 shapes in each zone

ShapeIdeaPresent example
SimpleA fact, habit or finished actionI write SQL queries.
ContinuousIn progress at that timeI am writing a query now.
PerfectFinished before a time; result mattersI have written the query.
Perfect continuousIn progress for a period up to a timeI have been writing queries all morning.
KEY IDEA
Learn the present zone first. Past and future copy the same four shapes. Only the helper verb changes.

17.3 The Four Present Tenses

The present tenses describe facts, routines, things in progress now, and things that started before now and still matter. Each tense below has a formula, uses, signal words and the mistakes learners make most.

Present Simple

BASIC EXPLANATION
Present Simple: The present simple is used for facts, habits, routines and things that are always true. It does not show an action happening this second.
INTUITION
A photo of your normal life. It is what is true on a normal day.

Form

  • Positive: subject + V1 (add -s/-es for he, she, it): She works.
  • Negative: subject + do/does not + V1: She does not work.
  • Question: Do/Does + subject + V1? Does she work?

When to use

  • Facts and general truths: A model learns from data.
  • Habits and routines: I check the dashboard every morning.
  • Schedules: The stand-up starts at 10 a.m.
  • Describing your role: I work as a data analyst.

Signal words: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, every week, on Mondays

Common mistakes

WrongRight
She work on the pipeline.She works on the pipeline.
Does he works here?Does he work here?
I am checking the dashboard every day.I check the dashboard every day.

Present Continuous

BASIC EXPLANATION
Present Continuous: The present continuous is used for an action that is happening now, or for a temporary situation around now, or for a fixed plan in the near future.
INTUITION
A live video. The action has started and is not finished yet.

Form

  • Positive: subject + am/is/are + V-ing: I am testing.
  • Negative: subject + am/is/are not + V-ing.
  • Question: Am/Is/Are + subject + V-ing? Are you testing?

When to use

  • Happening now: I am running the query.
  • Temporary work: We are using a small dataset this week.
  • Changing situation: Our data is growing fast.
  • Planned future: I am meeting the client tomorrow.

Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, currently, today, this week, these days

Common mistakes

WrongRight
I am knowing the answer.I know the answer. (Stative verbs like know, want, need, understand, believe are not used in continuous.)
I working on a model now.I am working on a model now.
He is work today.He is working today.

Present Perfect

BASIC EXPLANATION
Present Perfect: The present perfect is used for an action that happened at an unspecified time in the past, or that started in the past and still connects to now. The result or experience matters now.
INTUITION
A past action that you carry into today, like a certificate in your pocket. You do not say when you got it. You show that you have it.

Form

  • Positive: subject + have/has + V3: I have built.
  • Negative: subject + have/has not + V3.
  • Question: Have/Has + subject + V3? Have you built a model?

When to use

  • Experience: I have worked with Python and SQL.
  • Recent result: I have fixed the bug.
  • Unfinished period: I have written three reports this week.
  • Since a point or for a period: I have worked here for four years.

Signal words: already, yet, just, ever, never, recently, so far, since, for, this week

Common mistakes

WrongRight
I have finished it yesterday.I finished it yesterday. (A clear past time needs the past simple.)
I am here since 2022.I have been here since 2022.
She have completed the task.She has completed the task.

Present Perfect Continuous

BASIC EXPLANATION
Present Perfect Continuous: The present perfect continuous is used for an action that started in the past and is still going on, or has just stopped, with focus on the time spent or the activity itself.
INTUITION
A film that started earlier and is still running. You stress how long it has been running.

Form

  • Positive: subject + have/has been + V-ing: I have been learning.
  • Negative: subject + have/has not been + V-ing.
  • Question: Have/Has + subject + been + V-ing?

When to use

  • Still going: I have been learning Spark for two months.
  • Just stopped, with a visible result: I have been debugging, so I am tired.
  • Recent repeated activity: We have been testing the model all week.
  • Asking how long: How long have you been using Python?

Signal words: for, since, all day, all week, lately, recently, how long

Common mistakes

WrongRight
I am learning Spark since January.I have been learning Spark since January.
I have been knowing him for years.I have known him for years. (know is a stative verb.)
How long you are working here?How long have you been working here?

17.4 The Four Past Tenses

The past tenses describe things that finished or were in progress before now. In interviews, you use them to describe your experience and your stories. Past tenses are the most important group for storytelling.

Past Simple

BASIC EXPLANATION
Past Simple: The past simple is used for an action that started and finished at a definite time in the past. The time is known or clear.
INTUITION
A dated photo in an album. The event is closed and over.

Form

  • Positive: subject + V2: I built, I worked.
  • Negative: subject + did not + V1: I did not build.
  • Question: Did + subject + V1? Did you build it?

When to use

  • Finished event with a time: I deployed the model last Friday.
  • Series of events: I cleaned the data, trained the model and shared the result.
  • Past habit: I wrote reports every day in my first job.
  • Telling a story in an interview: We faced a problem. I found the cause.

Signal words: yesterday, last week, last year, in 2023, ago, then, when, earlier

Common mistakes

WrongRight
I did not built the model.I did not build the model. (After did, use the base verb.)
Did you went to the meeting?Did you go to the meeting?
I have joined the company in 2021.I joined the company in 2021.

Past Continuous

BASIC EXPLANATION
Past Continuous: The past continuous is used for an action that was in progress at a certain time in the past, or that was in progress when another action happened.
INTUITION
A video paused at a past moment. You see the action in the middle.

Form

  • Positive: subject + was/were + V-ing: I was testing.
  • Negative: subject + was/were not + V-ing.
  • Question: Was/Were + subject + V-ing?

When to use

  • In progress at a time: At 5 p.m. I was fixing the pipeline.
  • Interrupted action: I was running the job when the server crashed.
  • Two actions together: She was cleaning data while I was writing the report.
  • Background in a story: We were growing fast, so the old system was slowing down.

Signal words: while, when, as, at that time, at 5 p.m. yesterday, all day

Common mistakes

WrongRight
I was run the job when it failed.I was running the job when it failed.
When I was joining, the team was small.When I joined, the team was small. (A single finished action uses past simple.)
They was testing it.They were testing it.

Past Perfect

BASIC EXPLANATION
Past Perfect: The past perfect is used for an action that was finished before another action or time in the past. It shows the earlier one of two past events.
INTUITION
The past of the past. Two past events: the first one gets the past perfect.

Form

  • Positive: subject + had + V3: I had finished.
  • Negative: subject + had not + V3.
  • Question: Had + subject + V3?

When to use

  • Earlier event: When I joined, the team had already built the pipeline.
  • Reason for a past result: The job failed because someone had deleted the table.
  • Before a time: I had finished the report by 6 p.m.
  • Reported past: He said he had checked the data.

Signal words: before, after, already, by the time, when, until, just, never ... before

Common mistakes

WrongRight
When I arrived, the meeting already started.When I arrived, the meeting had already started.
I had finished and then I have sent it.I had finished, and then I sent it.
I had went home.I had gone home.

Past Perfect Continuous

BASIC EXPLANATION
Past Perfect Continuous: The past perfect continuous is used for a long action that was going on up to a point in the past, with focus on how long, or on the cause of a past situation.
INTUITION
A film that ran for some time and stopped just before a past moment.

Form

  • Positive: subject + had been + V-ing: I had been working.
  • Negative: subject + had not been + V-ing.
  • Question: Had + subject + been + V-ing?

When to use

  • Duration before a past point: I had been learning SQL for a year before I got the job.
  • Cause of a past situation: The server was slow because we had been running heavy jobs.
  • Tiredness: He was tired because he had been debugging all night.
  • Story background: We had been waiting for data for two weeks when it finally arrived.

Signal words: for, since, before, until, all day, how long ... before

Common mistakes

WrongRight
I had been know her before.I had known her before.
I was working for 3 years before I moved.I had been working for 3 years before I moved.
She had been wait for an hour.She had been waiting for an hour.

17.5 The Four Future Tenses

The future tenses describe plans, promises, predictions and deadlines. At work, you use them for status updates, estimates and commitments.

Future Simple

BASIC EXPLANATION
Future Simple: The future simple is used for decisions made at the moment of speaking, predictions, promises, offers and facts about the future.
INTUITION
A spoken promise or guess. You decide or predict now, without a fixed plan.

Form

  • Positive: subject + will + V1: I will send.
  • Negative: subject + will not (won't) + V1.
  • Question: Will + subject + V1?

When to use

  • Quick decision: I will check the logs now.
  • Promise or offer: I will send the report by 5 p.m.
  • Prediction: This model will perform better with more data.
  • Future fact: The release will be next Monday.

Signal words: tomorrow, next week, soon, later, in two days, probably, I think, I promise

Common mistakes

WrongRight
I will to send it.I will send it.
If it will rain, we stop.If it rains, we will stop. (After if, use the present simple.)
I will sent the file.I will send the file.

Future Continuous

BASIC EXPLANATION
Future Continuous: The future continuous is used for an action that will be in progress at a certain time in the future. It also sounds polite when you ask about someone's plans.
INTUITION
A video you can already see in the future: at that moment the action will be in the middle.

Form

  • Positive: subject + will be + V-ing: I will be testing.
  • Negative: subject + will not be + V-ing.
  • Question: Will + subject + be + V-ing?

When to use

  • In progress at a future time: At 3 p.m. tomorrow I will be presenting to the client.
  • Planned background work: We will be migrating the data this weekend.
  • Polite question: Will you be joining the call?
  • Availability: I will be working from home on Friday.

Signal words: at this time tomorrow, next week at 10, this time next month, all day tomorrow

Common mistakes

WrongRight
At 3 p.m. I will present when you call.At 3 p.m. I will be presenting when you call.
I will be know the result.I will know the result.
Will you joining the call?Will you be joining the call?

Future Perfect

BASIC EXPLANATION
Future Perfect: The future perfect is used for an action that will be finished before a certain time in the future.
INTUITION
Stand at a future deadline and look back: the work is already done by then.

Form

  • Positive: subject + will have + V3: I will have finished.
  • Negative: subject + will not have + V3.
  • Question: Will + subject + have + V3?

When to use

  • Deadline: I will have finished the report by Friday.
  • Milestone: By December, we will have launched three models.
  • Career goal: By 2028, I will have completed two projects and a certification.
  • Check: Will you have reviewed the code by noon?

Signal words: by, by then, by the time, before, by next week, by the end of the year

Common mistakes

WrongRight
I will finish by Friday and then it is done.I will have finished it by Friday.
By next year I will have been finished.By next year I will have finished.
I will have went.I will have gone.

Future Perfect Continuous

BASIC EXPLANATION
Future Perfect Continuous: The future perfect continuous is used to say how long an action will have been going on at a certain time in the future. It is the least common tense.
INTUITION
Stand at a future date and look back at a long action that has been running up to that date.

Form

  • Positive: subject + will have been + V-ing: I will have been working.
  • Negative: subject + will not have been + V-ing.
  • Question: Will + subject + have been + V-ing?

When to use

  • Length of time: By June, I will have been working here for five years.
  • Learning: Next month, I will have been studying machine learning for a year.
  • Project: By the launch, we will have been testing for six weeks.
  • Question: How long will you have been using Spark by then?

Signal words: by, by then, by next year, for + time, by the time

Common mistakes

WrongRight
By June I will be working here for five years.By June I will have been working here for five years.
I will have been work for years.I will have been working for years.
By May I will have been finish it.By May I will have finished it.

17.6 Summary Table of All 12 Tenses

BASIC EXPLANATION
The summary table puts all 12 tenses in one place. Read it across to compare shapes. Read it down to compare time zones.
INTUITION
It is like a multiplication table: 3 time zones x 4 shapes. If you remember the pattern, you do not need to remember 12 separate rules.
TenseFormulaExample
Present simpleV1 (+s)I write code every day.
Present continuousam/is/are + V-ingI am writing code now.
Present perfecthave/has + V3I have written the code.
Present perfect continuoushave/has been + V-ingI have been writing code since morning.
Past simpleV2I wrote the code yesterday.
Past continuouswas/were + V-ingI was writing code at 5 p.m.
Past perfecthad + V3I had written the code before the meeting.
Past perfect continuoushad been + V-ingI had been writing code for two hours.

The pattern behind the table

  • Simple = main verb alone (V1, V2) or with will.
  • Continuous = a form of be + V-ing.
  • Perfect = a form of have + V3.
  • Perfect continuous = a form of have + been + V-ing.

Verbs that do not like the continuous

Stative verbs describe states, not actions. Do not use them in continuous tenses in their normal meaning: know, understand, believe, want, need, like, love, prefer, own, belong, seem, mean. Say "I understand", not "I am understanding".

17.7 How to Choose the Right Tense

BASIC EXPLANATION
To choose a tense, ask three short questions: When is it (past, present, future)? Is it finished, in progress, or linked to another time? Do I care about how long?
INTUITION
It is like choosing the right tool. First you pick the time zone, then the shape. After two or three practice weeks, the choice becomes automatic.

A simple decision guide

  1. Find the time. Is it before now, now, or after now?
  2. Is the time clear and finished? (yesterday, in 2023, last week) Use the past simple.
  3. Is it a fact, habit or routine? Use the present simple.
  4. Is it in progress at that time? Use a continuous tense for that time zone.
  5. Is the action done before another time, and the result or experience matters? Use a perfect tense.
  6. Do you stress how long it has been going on? Use a perfect continuous tense.
  7. Is it a plan, promise or prediction? Use will (or going to or the present continuous for fixed plans).
Your ideaTenseExample
Fact or habitPresent simpleI review code every Monday.
Happening nowPresent continuousI am reviewing code now.
Experience or result nowPresent perfectI have reviewed 20 pull requests.
How long up to nowPresent perfect continuousI have been reviewing code since 9.
Finished, with a past timePast simpleI reviewed it yesterday.
In progress in the pastPast continuousI was reviewing when you called.
Earlier of two past eventsPast perfectI had reviewed it before the meeting.
Promise or decision nowFuture simpleI will review it today.
In progress at a future timeFuture continuousI will be reviewing at 4.
Done by a future timeFuture perfectI will have reviewed it by noon.

Present perfect or past simple?

  • Use past simple when the time is stated: I fixed it yesterday.
  • Use present perfect when the time is not stated and the result matters now: I have fixed it. You can test it.

Will, going to, or present continuous for the future?

FormUseExample
willDecision now, promise, predictionI will help you.
going toPlan already made, or sign you can seeI am going to learn Spark this year.
present continuousFixed arrangement with a timeI am meeting the manager at 4.
COMMON MISTAKE
Using one tense for everything (usually the present simple). The listener cannot tell what is finished, what is in progress and what is planned. Choose the tense on purpose.

17.8 Tenses in Interviews and at Work

BASIC EXPLANATION
In interviews and at work, each task has a usual tense. Interview stories use past tenses. Your current role and skills use present tenses. Plans and commitments use future tenses.
INTUITION
Think of a story with three parts. The past is what you did. The present is who you are now. The future is what you will do for them. Keep each part in its own tense.

Experience: have worked, worked, have been working

SentenceMeaningWhen to use
I worked at Infosys for three years.Finished. I do not work there now.Past job with a clear period
I have worked at Infosys for three years.Not finished. I still work there.Current job and total time
I have been working at Infosys for three years.Not finished. Focus on the time and the activity.Current job, stress on the long period
I have worked with Python and SQL.Experience, no time given.Listing skills in an interview
I worked with Python on a churn project in 2023.One specific past project.Giving a project example
KEY IDEA
"I worked" = finished. "I have worked" or "I have been working" = connected to now. Tell the interviewer the truth with the right tense.

Interview answers

QuestionTense patternModel answer
Tell me about yourself.Present simple, present perfect, past simple, future simpleI am a data analyst. I have four years of experience with SQL and Python. Last year I built a churn dashboard. I want to move into machine learning, and I will bring strong data skills.
Tell me about a project.Past simple, past continuous, past perfectWe were losing customers. I analysed the data and found the cause. The team had not checked the late deliveries before. I fixed the report, and churn fell by 8%.
What are you learning now?Present continuous, present perfect continuousI am learning Spark. I have been practising for two months.
Where do you see yourself?Future simple, future perfectI will lead a small team. By then I will have completed my ML certification.

Stand-ups and status updates

PartTenseExample
Yesterday (done)Past simpleYesterday I finished the data cleaning.
Recent resultPresent perfectI have fixed the null values.
Today (in progress)Present continuousToday I am building the features.
PlanFuture simple or going toI will start model training tomorrow.
BlockerPresent simple / present perfectI need access to the table. I have not received it yet.

A full stand-up in 4 sentences:

REAL JOB
"Yesterday I cleaned the sales data. Today I am building the features. I have one blocker: I have not received the access yet. I will finish the first model by Friday."

Emails

PurposeTenseExample
Reason for writingPresent simple or present continuousI am writing to ask about the report.
Reference to earlier mailPresent perfect or past simpleI have attached the file. As I mentioned yesterday, the data was late.
RequestPresent simple with could/canCould you share the access?

Describing a project on your resume or LinkedIn

  • Finished project: past simple. Built a model that reduced errors by 12%.
  • Current project: present simple or present continuous. Building a recommendation engine in Python.
  • Current role bullets: present simple is acceptable. Maintain 20 dashboards.
  • Keep one style in the same job: past for old jobs, present for the current one.
COMMON MISTAKE
Mixing tenses in one story without a reason, for example "I go to the client and I explained the problem". Choose one main tense (usually past simple for a story) and change only when the time really changes.

17.9 Conditionals and Reported Speech (Brief)

BASIC EXPLANATION
A conditional sentence has an if part (the condition) and a result part. Reported speech tells what someone said without using their exact words.
INTUITION
A conditional is a small experiment: if this happens, then that happens. Reported speech is a news report: you move the speaker's words one step back in time.

Four common conditionals

TypeUseFormExample
ZeroFacts that are always trueIf + present simple, present simpleIf the data is missing, the model fails.
FirstReal, possible futureIf + present simple, will + V1If you share the data, I will start today.
SecondUnreal or unlikely present/futureIf + past simple, would + V1If I had more data, I would train a bigger model.
ThirdUnreal past, a regretIf + past perfect, would have + V3If we had tested earlier, we would have found the bug.
COMMON MISTAKE
Using will after if: "If it will fail, call me." Right: "If it fails, call me."

Reported speech

When you report words from the past, the tense usually moves one step back.

Direct speechReported speech
"I am testing the model."She said she was testing the model.
"I have finished the report."He said he had finished the report.
"I finished it yesterday."He said he had finished it the day before.
"I will send it tomorrow."She said she would send it the next day.
"Do you use Spark?"He asked if I used Spark.

In reported questions, the word order is normal: He asked where I worked (not "where did I work"). If the fact is still true, you may keep the present: She said the deadline is Friday.

Useful polite forms

  • Polite request: Could you check this? / Would you mind sharing the file?
  • Soft opinion: I think we should wait. / I was wondering if we could meet.
  • Wish: I wish I had more time.

Chapter recap

  • A tense shows when an action happens (past, present, future) and its shape (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous).
  • 3 time zones x 4 shapes = 12 tenses. The shapes repeat in every zone.
  • Simple = fact, habit or finished event. Continuous = in progress. Perfect = finished, with a link to another time. Perfect continuous = in progress for some time up to a point.
  • Formulas: V1 / V2 / will + V1; be + V-ing; have + V3; have + been + V-ing.
  • Past simple needs a finished time. Present perfect has no stated time and the result matters now.
  • Stative verbs (know, want, need, understand) do not use the continuous.
  • After if, use the present simple for the future: "If it fails, call me."
  • "I worked" is finished. "I have worked" and "I have been working" are linked to now.
  • Interview stories are in the past simple; skills in the present perfect; current role in the present simple; plans in the future simple.
  • Reported speech usually moves the tense one step back.

Result or experiencePresent perfect

Finished with a timePast simple
Earlier of two past eventsPast perfect
Promise or decisionwill + V1
Done by a deadlinewill have + V3

Practice set

1. (Easy) Choose the right form: "She ___ (work) on the dashboard every day." Answer: works (present simple, habit, she needs -s).

2. (Easy) Choose: "Please wait. I ___ (finish) the report now." Answer: am finishing (present continuous, happening now).

3. (Easy) Make it negative: "He built the model." Answer: He did not build the model. After did not, use the base verb.

4. (Easy) Choose: "I ___ here since 2022." (work) Answer: have worked or have been working (since + still true).

5. (Medium) Correct the sentence: "I have joined this company in 2021." Answer: I joined this company in 2021. A finished time needs the past simple.

6. (Medium) Join the two events with the right tenses: (1) the meeting started, (2) I arrived. Meeting first. Answer: When I arrived, the meeting had started. The earlier event uses the past perfect.

7. (Medium) Choose: "At 4 p.m. tomorrow I ___ (present) to the client." Answer: will be presenting (future continuous, in progress at a future time).

8. (Medium) Choose: "By Friday I ___ (finish) the model." Answer: will have finished (future perfect, done by a deadline).

9. (Medium) What is the difference: "I worked at TCS for 2 years" and "I have worked at TCS for 2 years"? Answer: The first is finished (I left). The second is not finished (I still work there).

10. (Medium) Correct: "If it will rain, the match will stop." Answer: If it rains, the match will stop. After if, use the present simple.

11. (Medium) Report this: She said, "I am testing the model." Answer: She said she was testing the model. The tense moves one step back.

12. (Medium) Correct: "I am knowing the answer." Answer: I know the answer. Know is a stative verb.

13. (Interview) "Tell me about a project you led." Write the first 3 sentences in the right tenses. Answer: Model: "Last year I led a churn project. We were losing 5% of customers every month. I analysed the data and found that late deliveries were the main cause." (Past simple for events, past continuous for background.)

14. (Interview) Answer "How long have you been working in data?" in 2 sentences. Answer: Model: "I have been working in data for four years. I have worked mostly with SQL and Python."

15. (Scenario) Write a 4-sentence stand-up update: you cleaned data yesterday, you are building features today, you have a blocker (no access yet), you will finish by Friday. Answer: Model: "Yesterday I cleaned the sales data. Today I am building the features. I have a blocker: I have not received the access yet. I will finish the first model by Friday."

16. (Scenario) Fix this email line: "I am sending the report yesterday, please to check." Answer: I sent the report yesterday. Please check it. Or: I sent you the report yesterday. Could you please check it?

17. (Medium) Fill in the conditional: "If I ___ (have) more data, I ___ (train) a bigger model." Answer: had, would train (second conditional, unreal present).

18. (Medium) Choose the right tense: "I ___ (not / see) the error before you showed me." Answer: had not seen (past perfect, earlier of two past events).
