Job Search in India

A job search is a funnel you can measure, improve and run calmly. It is not a lottery.

You have a resume (Chapter 11) and interview skills (Chapter 7). Now you must put them in front of the right people and turn interviews into an offer you are happy with. This chapter shows how to treat the search as a pipeline with numbers, where referrals beat cold applications, which type of company fits which goal, how to move inside your own company, how to research salary without guessing, how to negotiate with facts, how to plan your notice period, and how to keep a rejection log that makes each failure useful. By the end you will have a weekly routine and a tracking sheet. All money figures in this chapter are made-up practice numbers. Salaries change with city, year and company, so I will show you how to find real data, not give you a number to quote.

12.1 The Search as a Funnel

INTUITION
Think of a shop with several stages: enter, browse, fill a basket, pay. Not every visitor reaches the till. If 1,000 walk in and 16 pay, you do not blame the till alone. You look at where visitors drop. A job search is the same: applications drop at the resume screen, the technical round, the final round.

Words you need

TermMeaning
FunnelA chain of stages where only some candidates pass each stage
Conversion rateThe share that passes a stage: passed / entered
ATSApplicant Tracking System, software that stores and filters resumes
ReferralA current employee recommends you; your resume goes to a person, not only a pile
PipelineAll your live applications at all stages right now

Read the picture top to bottom. Each bar is a stage, and the number is how many candidates are still alive. The percentages are the share that passed the previous stage. The note at the bottom says the rates are illustrative. Your own rates will differ, and you should measure them.

Problem 12.1.1 Start with 60 applications. Stage pass rates: screen 25%, technical 40%, final 33%, offer 50%. How many offers do you expect?

Formulacount after a stage = count before x pass rate
Plug in60 x 0.25 x 0.40 x 0.33 x 0.50
Compute60 x 0.25 = 15; 15 x 0.40 = 6; 6 x 0.33 = 1.98 (about 2); 2 x 0.50 = 1
AnswerAbout 1 offer from 60 applications.
CheckOverall rate = 0.25 x 0.40 x 0.33 x 0.50 = 0.0165. Then 60 x 0.0165 = 0.99, about 1. The code below prints the same.
Why it mattersPlain words: if you send 10 applications and hear nothing, that is normal. It is not a verdict on you.
stages=[("applied",1.0),("screen",0.25),("technical",0.40),("final",0.33),("offer",0.50)]
n=60
for name,r in stages:
    n*= r if name!="applied" else 1
    print(f"{name:10s} rate={r:.2f} count={n:.1f}")
p=0.25*0.40*0.33*0.50
print("overall rate",round(p,4),"apps per offer",round(1/p,1))

Before you run it, what do you think the last line prints? Here is the real output:

applied    rate=1.00 count=60.0
screen     rate=0.25 count=15.0
technical  rate=0.40 count=6.0
final      rate=0.33 count=2.0
offer      rate=0.50 count=1.0
overall rate 0.0165 apps per offer 60.6

Line by line: each loop step multiplies the running count by that stage's rate. The last line uses 1/p because if 1.65 in 100 applications become offers, you need 100 / 1.65, about 60.6, applications per offer.

Problem 12.1.2 Improve the first stage. A referral raises the screen rate from 25% to 60% (a practice number, not a promise). Later stages stay the same. How many applications do you need for one offer?

Plug inoverall rate = 0.60 x 0.40 x 0.33 x 0.50
Compute0.60 x 0.40 = 0.24; 0.24 x 0.33 = 0.0792; 0.0792 x 0.50 = 0.0396
Answer1 / 0.0396 = 25.3 applications, against 60.6 without referrals.
CheckScreen rate rose 2.4 times (0.60 / 0.25), so applications needed should fall by about 2.4 times: 60.6 / 2.4 = 25.3. Yes.
Why it mattersThe first stage is the cheapest to improve with a relationship. Interview skill lifts later stages, but that takes weeks of practice.

The picture: where to fix what

Low screen rate        -> resume, headline, channel (get referrals)
Good screen, no tech   -> technical prep (Chapter 7)
Good tech, no final    -> system design, communication, stories
Good final, no offer   -> negotiation, fit, compensation expectations

Connection to the real job

Treat your search like a small experiment: define a goal (for example 5 quality applications a week), measure each stage, and change one thing at a time. A weak funnel stage tells you what to practise.

Applications

  • Data Analyst search: resume keyword fit matters most at the first stage.
  • Data Engineer and ML Engineer search: technical-round pass rate often limits you.
  • Internal transfer: the funnel has fewer stages but the same logic.
COMMON MISTAKE
Judging the search after 8 applications. With rates like these the result is mostly noise at small numbers. Look at 30 or more applications before drawing a conclusion, and look at stage rates, not the final result only.

Check yourself

  1. If 40 of 100 pass screen and 10 of those 40 pass the technical round, what is the technical conversion? Answer: 10 / 40 = 25%.
  2. Which stage is cheapest to improve with a person you know? Answer: the first screen, through a referral.
  3. Why measure stage rates and not only offers? Answer: they show which skill to fix.

12.2 Channels: Referrals, Platforms and Outreach

INTUITION
A cold application is a letter dropped into a big postbox. A referral is a letter handed to the manager by a colleague he trusts. Both can work, but the second is read first.

The channels

ChannelHow it worksYour effort
ReferralAn employee submits you through the company's referral systemHighest value; needs relationships
LinkedInJobs, recruiters, and direct messages to engineers and managersDaily 15 minutes
Company career pagesDirect apply; some roles are posted only hereWeekly check of 10 target companies
Naukri, Instahyre, Hirist, Cutshort, WellfoundJob and recruiter platforms; Wellfound leans to startupsKeep profile current; respond to recruiters
CommunitiesDataTalks.Club Slack, local meetups, Kaggle and GitHub circlesGive help first
Recruiters and consultantsThird-party recruiters hold roles for client companiesReply politely; never pay a fee to get a job

Platform names and features change. Check each site yourself and do not assume what was true last year is true now.

REAL JOB TIP
A genuine recruiter never asks you to pay money for a job, an interview or a "registration". If someone does, stop and report it. Also be careful with unknown links and requests for identity documents before an official offer.

How to ask for a referral (a template)

A referral request must be easy to say yes to. Give the person the job link, two lines about you, and a way out.

Hi Priya, I saw you work on the data platform team at <Company>.
I am a working professional with 6 years of experience in IT moving into data engineering.
I built <one project, one number>. There is an open role: <link>.
Would you be comfortable referring me? If it is not a good fit, no problem at all.
I have attached my resume.

Cold outreach to a hiring manager

Hi Arjun, I read your post about reducing pipeline delay. I did a similar analysis on test data
and reduced report time from 40 to 6 minutes (repo: <link>). I am applying for the Data Engineer
role on your team. May I send you a 3-line summary of how my background fits?

Connection to the real job

Your network grows from the habits in Chapters 4 and 5: public posts, small projects, helpful answers. People refer those whose work they have seen.

Applications

  • Moving into a new role family: referrals reduce the "no direct experience" filter.
  • Switching city: recruiters and communities help when you lack a local network.
COMMON MISTAKE
Messaging a stranger "Please refer me" with no context. State the role, your proof, and make it easy to decline.

Check yourself

  1. Name two things a referral message must contain. Answer: the job link and a short proof of fit (resume or project).
  2. Should you pay a recruiter to get a job? Answer: no.
  3. Why give help in communities first? Answer: it builds trust that later makes referrals possible.

12.3 Company Types: Product, GCC, Service and Startup

INTUITION
Choosing a company type is like choosing a gym. All of them can make you fit, but the equipment, coaching and fees differ. Decide what you want first (learning, stability, money, speed), then choose.

Words you need

TermMeaning
Product companyBuilds and sells its own software or platform (for example an online marketplace, a payments app, a SaaS tool)
GCCGlobal Capability Center, an India team owned by a foreign company; also called captive centre
Service company or IT services MNCBuilds software for client companies; you work on client projects
StartupA young company, small team, fast change, less structure
Consulting firmAdvises clients on strategy and technology, often project and deadline driven

Comparison (general tendencies, not rules)

ProductGCCService MNCStartup
Work typeOwn product, long-term ownershipGlobal team's work, often real product workClient projects, varies widelyBroad, many hats
LearningDeep, if team is strongOften strong; depends on mandateDepends on project and clientFast, but self-driven
StabilityMedium to highMedium to high, tied to parent's plansMediumLower; funding risk
PayVaries; often higher with stockVaries; check per companyOften lower at the same level, but variesVaries; stock may be worth nothing
Interview styleCoding, design, case, bar-raiser roundsSimilar to parent's styleSkills screen, project-basedPractical task, founder chat

These are tendencies. A small team inside a large company can behave like a startup, and a service company can have an excellent AI team. Judge the team, not only the logo.

Problem 12.3.1 Choose using a weighted score. Your weights (sum 10): learning 4, pay 3, stability 2, flexibility 1. You score each option from 1 to 5 using your own research. Product: learning 5, pay 4, stability 3, flexibility 2. GCC: 4, 4, 4, 3. Startup: 5, 3, 2, 3. Compute each total.

Formulatotal = sum of weight x score
Product4x5 + 3x4 + 2x3 + 1x2 = 20 + 12 + 6 + 2 = 40
GCC4x4 + 3x4 + 2x4 + 1x3 = 16 + 12 + 8 + 3 = 39
Startup4x5 + 3x3 + 2x2 + 1x3 = 20 + 9 + 4 + 3 = 36
AnswerProduct 40, GCC 39, Startup 36. Product and GCC are almost tied, so the decision depends on the specific teams.
CheckMaximum possible = 10 x 5 = 50; all totals are below 50 and above 10. Yes.
Why it mattersPlain words: the method shows what you value; a gap of 1 point means "look closer", not "decided".

How to research a company (all free)

  • Read engineer posts and tech blogs of the company.
  • Look for the team on LinkedIn: who is there, how long they stay, what they did before.
  • Read employee reviews on AmbitionBox and Glassdoor with a calm mind: reviews are opinions, look for repeated points.
  • Ask your interviewers: "What does a good first year look like in this role?" and "How is success measured?"
  • Check for recent layoffs, funding news and leadership changes in public news.

Connection to the real job

Your first data job sets how fast you learn, so choose a team with real data and a mentor, even if pay is slightly less. A strong first move often beats a higher number in a weak team.

Applications

  • Career changers with a technical IT or testing background: platform, observability and data infrastructure teams in product firms and GCCs value your skill.
  • Early moves: service MNC to GCC or product is a common path.
COMMON MISTAKE
Treating a company type as a rule ("startups pay less", "GCCs are safe"). It is a tendency. Check the team.

Check yourself

  1. What does GCC stand for? Answer: Global Capability Center.
  2. Which one tends to have the highest funding risk? Answer: startup.
  3. Should you judge only by company logo? Answer: no, judge the team and the work.

12.4 The Internal Transfer Playbook

INTUITION
Your own company already trusts you. A new employer must guess. An internal move swaps "unknown outsider with a promise" for "known colleague with a track record".

The five steps

  1. Talk. Have coffee chats with data or analytics managers in your company. Ask what they need.
  2. Volunteer. Offer to help with small data tasks: a dashboard, a cleanup script, an analysis.
  3. Build one visible project. Example: an operations dashboard built from your own work data, with a one-page note on what it saved.
  4. Ask for a shadow assignment. A few hours a week on the data team's work, agreed with your manager.
  5. Formalise. Ask your manager and HR how internal job postings and transfers work, and apply through the official route.
REAL JOB TIP
Tell your current manager early, but frame it as growth: "I want to apply my current analytical work to data. Can I run a small project for our team?" Do not hide your plans, but never make threats about leaving.

Problem 12.4.1 Your project reduces report preparation from 3 hours to 20 minutes, 4 times a month, for a team of 1. How many hours per month and per year does it save?

Plug inold 3 h = 180 min; new 20 min; saved per run = 180 - 20 = 160 min
Computeper month 160 x 4 = 640 min = 10.67 h; per year 10.67 x 12 = 128 h
AnswerAbout 10.7 hours a month and 128 hours a year.
Check640 / 60 = 10.67; 640 x 12 = 7,680 min; 7,680 / 60 = 128. Yes.
Why it mattersA number like "128 hours a year" persuades a manager better than "I built a dashboard".

Problem 12.4.2 Compare the cost of the new-employer path and the internal path in months: new path needs 2 months of search + 2 months of notice = 4 months before joining. Internal path: 3 months of project + 1 month of transfer process = 4 months, but you keep salary and benefits and test the work first. Which has less risk?

Step 1Time is the same, 4 months.
Step 2Internal path: no notice period, no background verification, no probation reset (check your company policy), and you can stop if you dislike the work.
AnswerThe internal path has lower risk, even if the time is equal. It may give lower salary growth, so research both.
CheckRisk list is longer for the external path: new probation, new team, unknown culture.

Connection to the real job

Even if you later change company, the internal project becomes your first data resume bullet with a real number.

Applications

  • Large IT firms and GCCs usually have an internal job portal; ask HR how it works.
  • Small firms: propose a part-time hybrid role.
COMMON MISTAKE
Waiting to be "noticed". Managers are busy. Show a small finished project and ask.

Check yourself

  1. What is the first step of the playbook? Answer: talk to data or analytics managers.
  2. Name one advantage of an internal move. Answer: your track record is known, and risk is lower.
  3. How should you describe it to your manager? Answer: as growth, and by offering a project for the team.

12.5 Salary Research and Negotiation

INTUITION
Negotiation is like pricing a used car. You do not guess the value; you measure it. You collect data from several sources, then state a range you can defend.

Words you need

TermMeaning
CTCCost to Company, the total yearly amount the company spends on you; not the same as money in hand
Fixed payThe part paid regularly, in your salary
Variable payA bonus that depends on performance; may be paid at less than 100%
In-handMoney that arrives in your bank account after deductions
ESOP / RSUEmployee stock options / Restricted stock units, a right to company shares; value depends on the company
Joining bonusA one-time payment; often with a clause to repay it if you leave early
Total compensationFixed + expected variable + stock value + bonuses, per year

Where to find data

  • AmbitionBox, Glassdoor, levels.fyi: self-reported salaries. Treat each as a rough range. Samples are small, and users report in different ways (fixed or CTC).
  • Recruiters: ask "What is the budget range for this role?" It is a normal question.
  • Peers in your network: ask ranges, not exact figures.
  • Job posts: some postings show ranges.

Use at least three sources, discard extreme values and write a range in a sheet. Always ask: what exact role, how many years of experience, which city, and which year?

The negotiation script

  1. Let the company give the first number when possible. If asked early, say: "I am looking for a range that fits the role and market. Could you share the budget range?"
  2. If you must answer, give a range with a lower bound you accept, based on your research.
  3. When an offer arrives, say thank you, ask for the breakdown in writing (fixed, variable, stock, bonus), and ask for time to review.
  4. Ask once, politely, with evidence: "Based on market data and my experience, I was expecting something closer to X. Is there flexibility?"
  5. Negotiate more than the base: joining date, joining bonus, learning budget, level or title, remote days, review cycle.
INTERVIEW TIP
Never lie about your current salary. Companies can verify through documents or background checks. Instead say what you expect and why. Check local laws and the offer terms; if unsure, ask a trusted adviser.

Offer-letter checklist

  • Fixed, variable and stock in writing; vesting schedule for stock.
  • Notice period and buyout rules; non-compete and confidentiality clauses.
  • Joining bonus repayment clause and its length.
  • Probation length, location and work-from-office rules.
  • Background verification: what they check and when.

Connection to the real job

The same habit helps in yearly reviews: bring evidence and a market range, not feelings.

Applications

  • Job switch, internal promotion, freelance rate setting, and counter-offer decisions.
COMMON MISTAKE
Comparing CTC with CTC. One company counts insurance and gratuity inside CTC, another does not. Compare fixed pay, expected variable and stock separately. Consult a qualified adviser for tax questions.

Check yourself

  1. Why is fixed pay safer to compare? Answer: it is regular and guaranteed.
  2. What should you do when you receive an offer? Answer: thank them, ask for it in writing, and take time to review.
  3. Name two non-salary items you can negotiate. Answer: joining date and learning budget (or remote days, level).

12.6 Notice-Period Strategy

INTUITION
A notice period is like a handover window in an incident rotation. A good exit leaves the team safe and keeps your name clean, because your next reference may come from this team.

Words you need

TermMeaning
Notice periodTime between resignation and last working day, set in your contract (often 30, 60 or 90 days in India)
BuyoutPaying the company to shorten the notice period, if policy allows
Garden leaveYou are paid but not required to work during notice (rare)
Relieving letterOfficial document confirming you left properly; new employers often ask for it
Counter-offerYour current employer offers more money or a new role to keep you

Rules of thumb

  1. Read your contract before you apply. Know the exact days and buyout rules.
  2. Resign only after you have a signed offer letter and have cleared the background verification terms.
  3. Tell new employers your notice period honestly in the first call. Some prefer immediate joiners, and some will wait for the right candidate.
  4. Ask the new company: can the joining date be adjusted? Can they pay a buyout through a clause? (Many say no; ask politely, once.)
  5. Do a handover document: running tests, dashboards, scripts, contacts, open issues.
  6. Treat counter-offers carefully. Ask: why did it take my resignation to fix pay or growth?

Notice period tactics for the search

  • Start interviewing before you are tired, because a long notice means a long total timeline.
  • Keep a clean record. Do not criticise your employer in interviews.
  • Use notice for rest, not only for work: a gap of a few days before joining protects your energy.

Connection to the real job

References from your last team often matter more than the salary. A professional handover is a signal that you will be a good teammate.

Applications

  • Anyone with 60 or 90 days' notice, planning when to start applying.
COMMON MISTAKE
Resigning on a verbal "we will send the offer". Wait for the signed offer letter and for any background-check conditions in writing.

Check yourself

  1. When should you resign? Answer: after a signed offer letter.
  2. What is a buyout? Answer: paying to shorten the notice period.
  3. Why write a handover document? Answer: to leave the team safe and protect your reputation.

12.7 The Rejection Log and Improvement Cycle

INTUITION
A failed driving-test attempt is not only a failure; it is data. If you throw the data away, you will repeat the failure. A rejection log turns every "no" into the next study topic.

The log

Use a spreadsheet or a small database. Columns: company, role, date, channel (referral, portal, recruiter), stage reached, outcome, reason or question you struggled with, action, and date you fixed it.

Problem 12.7.1 Seven records are in the log. Count the rejection reasons.

import sqlite3
con=sqlite3.connect(":memory:")
con.execute("create table log(company text, role text, stage text, outcome text, reason text)")
rows=[("A","DE","screen","reject","no Spark exp"),("B","DE","tech","reject","SQL window func"),
("C","DA","tech","reject","SQL window func"),("D","DE","final","reject","system design"),
("E","DE","tech","reject","SQL window func"),("F","DE","screen","pass",""),("G","DE","tech","reject","system design")]
con.executemany("insert into log values(?,?,?,?,?)",rows)
for r in con.execute("select reason,count(*) c from log where outcome='reject' group by reason order by c desc"):print(r)
for r in con.execute("select stage,count(*) from log group by stage order by 2 desc"):print(r)

  • Step 2 The second counts how many times each stage appears in the log.

The improvement cycle

Apply -> Interview -> Log the questions you struggled with (same day)
   ^                                                          |
   |                                                          v
Retest yourself <- Study one weakness for 1-2 weeks <- Rank by count

Rules for the cycle:

  1. Write the log the same day, while you remember the questions.
  2. Rank reasons by count. Fix the top one first.
  3. Retest with a mock interview (Chapter 7).
  4. Ask recruiters for feedback; some give it, many do not. Do not argue.
  5. Review every 2 weeks and update your funnel numbers from 12.1.

Handling rejection emotionally

A rejection says "not this team, not this day". It does not say "you cannot do this work". See Chapter 14 for feelings of self-doubt. Take a short break after three rejections in a row, then read your log and pick the next step.

Connection to the real job

Post-incident reviews work the same way: a blameless note of what happened, why, and what changes. You already know this habit.

Applications

  • Job search, promotion cases, and any exam or certification attempt.
COMMON MISTAKE
Not logging, then repeating the same mistake and calling it bad luck. Log within the day.

Check yourself

  1. What column in the log is the most useful? Answer: the reason or question you struggled with.
  2. How do you decide what to study next? Answer: count reasons and fix the biggest one.
  3. How often to update funnel numbers? Answer: about every two weeks.

Chapter recap

  • A job search is a funnel; measure stage conversion rates and fix the weakest stage.
  • Sixty applications at the sample rates gives about one offer; referrals can cut that number a lot.
  • Referrals are the cheapest way to raise the first-stage rate; make requests short and easy to decline.
  • Product, GCC, service and startup differ in tendencies; judge the team and score options using your own weights.
  • An internal transfer uses a visible project, a shadow assignment and the official route.
  • Research salary from at least three sources and compare total compensation, not CTC headlines; never invent or lie about numbers.
  • Resign only after a signed offer; read your notice terms before applying; write a handover.
  • Keep a rejection log, rank reasons by count and fix one weakness at a time.

Practice set

  1. (Easy) 100 applications, screen 20%, technical 50%, final 50%, offer 50%. How many offers? Answer: 100 x 0.2 x 0.5 x 0.5 x 0.5 = 2.5 offers.
  2. (Easy) Current fixed pay 10 lakh, new 13 lakh. Hike? Answer: 3 / 10 = 30%.
  3. (Easy) What is the difference between CTC and in-hand? Answer: CTC is the total yearly cost to the company; in-hand is what reaches your bank after deductions and non-cash items.
  4. (Medium) You send 25 messages and get 5 replies. Reply rate? Answer: 5 / 25 = 20%.
  5. (Medium) Offer: fixed 15, variable target 3 paid at 70%, joining bonus 1.5, no stock. Year-1 total? Answer: 15 + 3 x 0.7 + 1.5 = 15 + 2.1 + 1.5 = 18.6 lakh.
  6. (Medium) Find the bug in the plan: "I will resign today because the recruiter said the offer is coming." Answer: Resigning before a signed offer is risky; wait for the signed letter.
  7. (Medium) The log shows 10 rejections: 5 system design, 3 SQL, 2 DSA. What do you study first and why? Answer: System design, because it caused 5 of 10 (50%) of rejections.
  8. (Medium) Notice is 60 days, offer on 15 January 2027. Joining date if you resign that day? Answer: 15 Jan + 60 days = 16 March 2027 (January has 16 days left after the 15th, February has 28 in 2027, so 16 + 28 = 44, plus 16 in March = 60).
  9. (Hard) Offer A: fixed 18, nothing else. Offer B: fixed 15, variable 3 at 100%, stock 4 per year valued at half. Which is higher in expected yearly value? Answer: B = 15 + 3 + 2 = 20 versus A = 18, so B is higher on paper; but B is less certain, so judge risk too.
  10. (Interview) "What are your salary expectations?" Give a good answer. Answer: "I have researched market ranges for this role and level. I would like to understand the budget for this role. Based on my research I am looking for a range of X to Y, and I am open to discussing the full package." (Use your own researched range.)
  11. (Interview) "Why do you want to leave?" Answer: Speak about growth, not blame: "I have grown in my technical role and want to apply that analytical work to data systems. This role gives me that."
  12. (Hard, scenario) You have two offers with 90-day and 30-day notice and the 90-day firm is slightly better. What do you check? Answer: Whether the better firm can wait or start remote work earlier, whether the buyout is possible, and whether the gap in growth justifies three more months of notice.
