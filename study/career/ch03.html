CHAPTER 3

Spoken English for Technical People

Clear, simple, confident English beats fancy English. Your accent is not the problem. Clarity is the goal.

In every data job you will speak: in stand-ups, in meetings with business people, in interviews, and when you explain a result to a manager. Many strong engineers lose offers, not because they lack skill, but because the interviewer could not follow their explanation. This chapter gives you a daily 10-minute routine, a phrase bank you can copy, two simple structures for almost every answer, and a method for handling questions you cannot answer. By the end you will be able to give a 60-second status update and a clear two-minute explanation of a technical idea, and you will know how to practise alone.

3.1 The Daily Speaking Routine, Shadowing and Vocabulary

BASIC EXPLANATION
A daily speaking routine is regular practice of speaking aloud. Shadowing means repeating a speaker's words just after hearing them, and vocabulary work means learning the words used in data jobs. They are used to improve spoken English.
INTUITION
Speaking is a physical skill, like cycling or batting. You cannot learn it by reading about it. Your mouth and brain need repeated practice, and you need feedback. The good news: 10 minutes a day is enough to improve visibly in 3 months, because the practice is daily.

Words you need

WordMeaning
ShadowingPlaying a recording and repeating each sentence right after the speaker, copying rhythm and sound
RhythmWhich words are strong and which are soft in a sentence
FillerSounds like "umm", "you know" used while thinking
Phrase bankYour own notebook of ready-made sentences

The 10-minute routine

  1. Pick one concept (for example: "What is an index in a database?").
  2. Explain it aloud for 2 minutes as if to a beginner. Use your phone voice recorder.
  3. Listen to the recording once. Do not judge. Note only ONE thing to fix (too fast? many "umm"? a word you mispronounced?).
  4. Next day, explain a new concept and fix that one thing.
  5. Once a week, re-record an old topic and compare. You will hear the progress.

Shadowing (5 minutes, separate from the routine)

  1. Choose a short tech talk of 5 to 10 minutes with clear speech and subtitles.
  2. Listen to one sentence. Pause.
  3. Repeat it at the same speed, copying the rhythm and the stressed words.
  4. Do 10 sentences. Next day, shadow the same clip again, because repeating the same clip is useful.

Vocabulary: 5 phrases a week, in context

Do not memorise word lists. Take 5 phrases from your real work or a tech talk, write them in a sentence of your own, and put each on an Anki card (Chapter 2). Example: "We are blocked on access" (you cannot proceed because you lack permission).

Where to practise with people

  • Toastmasters clubs (many cities in India have them) or a local speaking group.
  • A language-exchange partner or a study buddy, with a weekly 20-minute call.
  • Optional paid apps and tutors (ELSA Speak, Cambly). Check current prices yourself; they change.
  • YouTube channels on English for tech professionals.

Problem 3.1.1 You speak at 110 words per minute (wpm) and a good explanation target is 130 wpm. Your 2-minute recording has 190 words. Is the problem speed or length?

Formulawords per minute = words / minutes
Plug in190 / 2
Compute190 / 2 = 95 wpm
AnswerYou speak at 95 wpm, slower than the 110 you thought, and well below 130. A slow pace is fine for clarity; the real issue is long pauses and fillers. Next day, fix one thing: remove long pauses by planning your first sentence.
CheckAt 130 wpm, 2 minutes is 260 words. You said 190, which is 190 / 260 = 73% of that. Matches "slower than target".
Why it mattersNumbers turn a vague feeling ("my English is bad") into one fixable thing.

Problem 3.1.2 You do the 10-minute routine for 5 days a week for 12 weeks. How many hours is that?

Formulatotal minutes = minutes per day x days per week x weeks
Plug in10 x 5 x 12
Compute10 x 5 = 50, and 50 x 12 = 600 minutes
Answer600 minutes = 10 hours. Ten hours of recorded practice in a quarter is more speaking practice than most people do in a year.
Check600 / 60 = 10 hours. Correct.

The picture

Day 1: explain ----> record ----> listen ----> pick ONE fix
                                                 |
Day 2: explain <----------------------------------+
        (apply the fix)           repeat every day

Read it as a loop. The arrow back from "pick ONE fix" to the next day is the whole method. One fix per day, not ten.

Connection to the real job

Stand-ups, demos and interviews are short spoken tests. A learner who has recorded 40 explanations is calm in them.

Applications

  • Data Analyst: explaining a dashboard to a sales head.
  • Data Engineer: explaining a pipeline failure to a manager in 30 seconds.
  • ML/AI Engineer: explaining model accuracy to a non-technical product owner.
COMMON MISTAKE
Trying to copy an American or British accent. Your goal is to be understood. Clear stress on the key word and a calm pace help more than any accent.

Check yourself

  1. How long is the routine? Answer: 10 minutes, one concept, one fix.
  2. What is shadowing? Answer: repeating a speaker's sentences right after them to copy rhythm.
  3. How many new phrases per week? Answer: 5, learned in context.

3.2 The Meeting Phrase Bank

BASIC EXPLANATION
A meeting phrase bank is a list of ready phrases for common moments in a meeting, such as agreeing, disagreeing, asking for clarity and giving updates. It is used to speak clearly without searching for words.
INTUITION
A chef does not invent a knife every time. He keeps a few good ones sharp. A phrase bank is your set of sharp knives: a few polite sentences you can use without thinking, so your brain is free for the technical part.

The phrase bank

SituationSay this
Agree"I agree with that." / "That makes sense to me."
Disagree politely"I see your point. Can I share a different view?" / "I am not sure that will work, because..."
Ask for clarification"Could you explain that a little more?" / "When you say 'users', do you mean all users or active users?"
Confirm understanding"So to confirm, you want X by Friday, is that right?"
Buy time"Let me check and get back to you." / "That is a good question. Give me a moment to think."
Say you do not know"I do not know yet. I will find out and reply by tomorrow."
Interrupt politely"Sorry to jump in, can I add one point?"
Ask for repetition"Sorry, could you say that again, a little slower?"
Give a status"I have finished A, I am working on B, I am blocked on C."
Raise a risk"One risk I see is... The impact would be... I suggest..."
Close a meeting"Let me summarise the actions: Ravi will do X by Monday, I will do Y by Wednesday."

Stand-up and status structure

A stand-up is a short daily meeting where each person says what they did. Use this order: what I did, the result, what is next, blockers. A blocker is anything that stops your work.

Sample 60-second stand-up (68 words, checked by code; at 130 wpm this is about 31 seconds):

"Yesterday I finished the SQL query for the weekly sales report and tested it on one month of data. The result matches the old report. Today I will add the region filter and send it for review. I have one blocker: I do not have access to the returns table. I have asked the data owner, and I will follow up this afternoon if there is no reply."

Problem 3.2.1 Turn this vague status into the four-part structure: "Working on the pipeline, some issues."

Step 1What I did: "I fixed the null-value error in the orders load."
Step 2Result: "The nightly run now finishes in 22 minutes instead of failing."
Step 3Next: "Today I will add a row-count check."
Step 4Blockers: "I need read access to the staging bucket; I raised a ticket yesterday."
Answer"I fixed the null-value error in the orders load. The nightly run now finishes in 22 minutes instead of failing. Today I will add a row-count check. I need read access to the staging bucket, and I raised a ticket yesterday."
CheckThe new status has 4 parts, 1 number, and a clear ask. The old one had none of these. (Use only real numbers.)
Why it mattersManagers who hear "some issues" must ask more questions; managers who hear the structure can help in 10 seconds.

Problem 3.2.2 A colleague asks, "Can you finish the report today?" You are not sure. Use the phrase bank to answer in two sentences, without promising or refusing.

Step 1Buy time: "Let me check my tasks and get back to you."
Step 2Give a time: "I will reply by 2 pm."
Answer"Let me check my tasks and get back to you. I will reply by 2 pm with a clear time."
CheckThe answer is honest (no false promise), polite and has a deadline. That matters more than a quick "yes" you cannot keep.

The picture

Meeting flow          Phrase to use
Listen             -> (nod, take notes)
Not clear?         -> "Could you explain that a little more?"
Understand?        -> "So to confirm, ..."
Need to disagree?  -> "I see your point. Can I share a different view?"
End                -> "Let me summarise the actions..."

Connection to the real job

Business people often use words loosely ("customer", "active"). The phrase "When you say X, do you mean...?" prevents weeks of wrong work. This is the first step of the problem-solving method in Chapter 6.

Applications

  • Analyst: confirming the definition of a metric before building a dashboard.
  • Data Engineer: negotiating deadlines with a data owner.
  • ML Engineer: raising a risk about an unrealistic accuracy target.
COMMON MISTAKE
Saying "yes" to everything to avoid conflict, then missing the deadline. A polite "Let me check and reply by 2 pm" builds more trust than a wrong yes.

Check yourself

  1. Give the four parts of a status. Answer: what I did, result, next, blockers.
  2. How do you buy time politely? Answer: "Let me check and get back to you."
  3. What does "So to confirm" do? Answer: It repeats the request to avoid misunderstanding.

3.3 Structuring Answers: PREP and STAR

BASIC EXPLANATION
PREP (Point, Reason, Example, Point) and STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) are fixed outlines for answers. They are used to give short, organised answers in meetings and interviews.
INTUITION
When you give directions in a city, you say "go straight, turn left at the temple, it is the blue gate." You do not start with the history of the road. A structure is a route: the listener always knows where you are and where you are going.

PREP for explanations

Point, Reason, Example, Point again. Say your answer first, then why, then a real case, then repeat the answer.

Sample (55 words):

"I would choose a median here. The reason is that salaries have a few very large values, and those pull the average up. For example, if five people earn 30, 32, 35, 36 and 400 thousand, the average is 106.6 thousand but the median is 35 thousand. So the median describes a typical person better."

STAR for behavioral questions

A behavioral question asks about your past ("Tell me about a time you..."). The company wants to see how you act. Situation, Task, Action, Result. Spend about 15% on Situation and Task, 60% on Action, 25% on Result. Say "I", not only "we", because they hire you.

Sample (66 words):

"At my company a new ordering page was due on Friday. On Wednesday, our timing check showed the checkout page taking 4 seconds at the 95th percentile. My task was to find the cause before Friday. I checked the database logs and saw one query without an index. I added the index and measured again. The 95th percentile fell to 1.6 seconds, and we released on time."

(Your own story must be true. Chapter 7 gives eight story templates.)

Problem 3.3.1 Check the sample numbers. The median of 30, 32, 35, 36, 400 and the average?

Step 1Sort: 30, 32, 35, 36, 400. Five values, so the median is the 3rd value.
Step 2Median = 35.
Step 3Sum = 30 + 32 + 35 + 36 + 400 = 533. Average = 533 / 5 = 106.6.
AnswerMedian is 35 thousand; average is 106.6 thousand. The one big value of 400 pulls the average to three times the median.
CheckRan in Python: (30+32+35+36+400)/5 gives 106.6.
Why it mattersThe example in the "E" of PREP must have real numbers or the listener will not believe it.

Problem 3.3.2 Rewrite this rambling answer to "Why SQL over Excel for 5 million rows?" in PREP: "Well, Excel is good, but there are limits, and I think databases are used in companies, and also..."

Step 1Point: "I would use SQL."
Step 2Reason: "Excel allows about 1 million rows per sheet, so 5 million rows will not even open."
Step 3Example: "In my last report, a query on 5 million rows ran in a few seconds in the database."
Step 4Point: "So for this size, SQL is the right tool."
Answer"I would use SQL. Excel allows about 1 million rows per sheet, so 5 million will not fit. For example, I ran a report on 5 million rows in a database and it finished in seconds. So SQL is the right tool here." (Excel's limit is 1,048,576 rows per sheet.)
Check5,000,000 / 1,048,576 = 4.8, so you would need five sheets. The reason holds.

Time and length

At about 130 words per minute: a 60-second answer is about 130 words, 2 minutes is about 260 words. A good interview answer is 1 to 2 minutes. A STAR story is 2 minutes.

Answer typeTargetWords at 130 wpm
"Tell me about yourself"90 secondsabout 195
Technical explanation (PREP)60 secondsabout 130
STAR story2 minutesabout 260
Stand-up update30 to 45 seconds65 to 100

The picture

PREP:  [Point] -> [Reason] -> [Example] -> [Point]
STAR:  [Situation 15%] [Task] -> [Action 60%] -> [Result 25%]
Status:[Did] -> [Result] -> [Next] -> [Blockers]

Read left to right. Each box is one or two sentences. If you cannot say what box you are in, you are rambling.

Connection to the real job

Interviewers give you 20 seconds of patience before they decide whether you are clear. Starting with the answer (Point) wins that 20 seconds.

Applications

  • Interviews: PREP for technical, STAR for behavioral.
  • Written updates: the same status structure works in Slack.
  • Stakeholder meetings: lead with the recommendation.
COMMON MISTAKE
Telling the whole backstory before the answer. Say the answer first. The listener can ask for the story if they want it.

Check yourself

  1. What does PREP stand for? Answer: Point, Reason, Example, Point.
  2. Which STAR part gets the most time? Answer: Action, about 60%.
  3. How many words is a 2-minute answer? Answer: about 260 at 130 wpm.

3.4 Handling Questions, Presenting, and Pronouncing Tech Words

BASIC EXPLANATION
Handling questions, presenting and pronouncing technical words are core speaking skills at work. They are used to answer calmly, present results and be understood when using terms such as SQL or API.
INTUITION
A question is not an attack. It is the other person asking for a map because the road was not clear. When you stay calm, you turn a hard moment into a conversation.

The Q&A routine: pause, restate, answer briefly, be honest

  1. Pause for 2 seconds. It looks calm and gives your brain time.
  2. Restate: "So you are asking how we handle late data, is that right?" This gives you more time and confirms the question.
  3. Answer briefly in PREP.
  4. If you do not know, say it honestly: "I do not know yet. My guess would be X because Y. I will find out and send you the answer." A reasoned guess plus honesty is stronger than silence or a bluff.

Presenting results: problem, approach, result, recommendation

Slide plan for a 5-minute talk (one message per slide):

SlideMessageTime
1The problem and why it matters (with a number)45 s
2Approach in 3 steps60 s
3Result: the one key chart90 s
4Recommendation and next step45 s
5Risks and questions60 s

Practise with a timer. If you take 7 minutes at home, you will take 8 on stage. Cut a slide.

Problem 3.4.1 A senior engineer asks in a design review: "What happens if the upstream file arrives two hours late?" You do not know. Write the reply.

Step 1Pause and restate: "So you are asking what happens to the downstream tables if the file is late."
Step 2Give what you do know: "The pipeline starts at 2 am and has no retry after 4 am."
Step 3Be honest: "I have not tested a two-hour delay. My guess is that the job fails and the dashboard shows yesterday's data."
Step 4Promise follow-up: "I will test it and share the result by Thursday."
AnswerA reply of four short sentences that restates the question, shares known facts, gives a labelled guess, and promises a date.
CheckIt has no bluff and it gives a next action. A bluff can be caught; honesty builds trust.

Problem 3.4.2 Plan a 5-minute presentation: you have 7 slides. Each slide takes 1 minute to say. Fix it.

Step 1Total time = 7 x 1 = 7 minutes, so you are 2 minutes over.
Step 2Remove two slides: merge approach details into one slide and move extra charts to an appendix.
AnswerKeep 5 slides at 1 minute each (or the table above). Extra material goes in a backup slide for Q&A.
Check5 x 1 = 5 minutes. In plain words: a short talk you finish is better than a long one you rush.

The picture

Question arrives -> [PAUSE 2 s] -> [RESTATE] -> [ANSWER brief]
                                                  |
                                          know it? yes -> PREP
                                                  no  -> honest guess + promise

Connection to the real job

In interviews, how you handle a question you cannot answer is often scored more than a perfect answer. In real work, saying "I will find out" is a professional habit.

Applications

  • Interview rounds (technical and behavioral).
  • Sprint demos and design reviews.
  • Customer or stakeholder calls where you represent your team.
COMMON MISTAKE
Guessing confidently without saying it is a guess. If a senior person finds the error, your trust drops. Say "my guess is..." clearly.

Check yourself

  1. What do you do first when you get a hard question? Answer: pause, then restate.
  2. What do you say when you do not know? Answer: "I do not know yet; here is my guess; I will find out."
  3. How many messages per slide? Answer: one.

3.5 Meeting Scripts: Five Moments Rehearsed

BASIC EXPLANATION
A meeting script is a prepared outline of what to say at a typical meeting moment, such as a stand-up update or a request for help. It is used to rehearse before the real meeting.
INTUITION
Speaking in a meeting is like batting in a fast over: you cannot invent a technique on the spot. You rehearse two or three shots until they come without thinking. Scripts are your rehearsed shots.

Sections 3.1 to 3.4 gave phrases and structures. Here are complete scripts for the five moments where learners most often freeze, each with a timing check and a self-score.

Problem 3.5.1 Write a 30-second stand-up update using did, result, next, blocker.

Draft"Yesterday I fixed the duplicate-order bug in the loader. Counts now match finance. Today I will add a daily count check. I am blocked on access to the finance table; I have asked Priya."
Word countAbout 40 words.
Time40 words at 120 words per minute = 20 seconds. Good.
CheckIt has did, result, next and blocker, and names one person for the blocker.

Problem 3.5.2 A manager asks, "Can you finish this by Friday?" and you cannot. Write the reply.

PatternAcknowledge, state the fact, offer options.
Script"I understand Friday matters. The cleaning step needs 3 more days and testing 1 day, so full delivery is next Wednesday. By Friday I can give you the top-line numbers without the regional split. Which do you prefer?"
Why it worksIt gives a date, a partial option and a choice, not only a no.
Check3 + 1 = 4 working days from Thursday gives next Wednesday only if Thursday is day 0 and the weekend is skipped: Fri, Mon, Tue, Wed. Yes.

Problem 3.5.3 You did not understand a question in a call. Write three lines you can use.

Line 1"Let me check I understood: you are asking whether the drop is in new or in returning users?"
Line 2"Could you give me one example?"
Line 3"I do not have the figure now. I will send it by 4 pm today."

COMMON MISTAKE
Translating word by word from your first language in your head. Wrong: long silence, then an unnatural sentence. Right: use a short bridge such as "Good question, let me think for a second" and then a simple sentence you know.

Check yourself

  1. 60 words spoken at 120 words per minute? Answer: 30 seconds.
  2. What follows "I cannot do it by Friday"? Answer: A reason with a fact and two options.
  3. Four parts of a stand-up? Answer: Did, result, next, blocker.

Chapter recap

  • Practise speaking 10 minutes a day: explain, record, listen, fix one thing.
  • Shadow a short tech talk to improve rhythm and sound.
  • Learn 5 phrases a week in context, not word lists.
  • Use a phrase bank for agreeing, disagreeing, clarifying and buying time.
  • Use PREP for explanations and STAR for behavioral stories.
  • Status updates: what I did, result, next, blockers.
  • On a hard question: pause, restate, answer briefly, be honest.
  • Rough speaking speed is about 130 words per minute; plan answer length from that.
  • Rehearse full scripts for stand-up, a deadline you cannot meet, and a question you did not understand (section 3.5).

Practice set

  1. (Easy) How many words is a 90-second answer at 130 wpm? Answer: 1.5 x 130 = 195 words.
  2. (Easy) Expand PREP. Answer: Point, Reason, Example, Point.
  3. (Easy) How do you say "cache"? Answer: "cash".
  4. (Medium) What is wrong with this status: "Working on stuff"? Answer: No result, no next step, no blockers, no number. Use the four-part status.
  5. (Medium) What is the output? A speaker uses 260 words in 2 minutes. Words per minute? Answer: 260 / 2 = 130 wpm.
  6. (Medium) Find the bug in this STAR answer: "We, as a team, decided..., we did..., we solved it." Answer: No "I". The interviewer cannot tell what you did. State your own actions.
  7. (Medium) Scenario: a colleague talks fast and you did not understand. What do you say? Answer: "Sorry, could you say that again, a little slower?"
  8. (Hard) Interview: "Tell me about yourself" in 90 seconds. Answer: Present (role and one number), past (how you got here: a technical role with some data analysis), future (target role and why). About 195 words. Practise aloud with a timer.
  9. (Hard) Interview: "What is your biggest weakness?" Answer: Name a real, fixable one, show action and progress. Example: "I used to avoid speaking in large meetings. I now record 10 minutes daily and give a weekly update to my team. I am more comfortable, and I still prepare my first sentence."
  10. (Hard) Scenario: in a meeting the VP asks a question outside your area. What do you say? Answer: "That is outside my area, so I do not want to guess. Anita on the platform team would know. I will ask her and reply by tomorrow."
  11. (Easy) A 90-word update at 120 words per minute: time? Answer: 90 / 120 = 0.75 minutes, 45 seconds.
  12. (Medium) Write a polite way to ask for a repeat. Answer: "Could you repeat the last part? I want to be sure I answer the right question."
  13. (Interview) A senior challenges your number in a meeting. What do you do? Answer: Stay calm, restate the definition and source, offer to recheck, and follow up in writing.
